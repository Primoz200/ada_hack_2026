# Validated digital motor tasks (tracing/spiral, tapping, reaction time, Fitts reaching, digital 9HPT) for stroke, CTS/carpal tunnel release, and CIPN

Scope note: this covers about 20 search and fetch calls made in Nov 2026. Several full texts on PMC and Springer were blocked by captcha or login, so some numbers come from abstracts (via Europe PMC) and search-result summaries. Where only the abstract was available, this is noted. Main finding: **the evidence base for these exact conditions is thin**. Tracing and spiral metrics are well validated in tremor (PD/ET). Touchscreen and tablet reaching is validated in stroke. For CTS and CIPN there are only small pilot or feasibility studies.

## 1. Tracing / spiral / drawing metrics: discrimination and correlation with clinical scales

### Takeaway
Digitized spiral drawing is psychometrically strong for **tremor** (r = 0.94 with clinical rating, ICC 0.97). Most of that evidence is in PD and ET, not stroke. In stroke, the best-validated tablet and touchscreen tasks are **reaching** tasks, not tracing. Their kinematic metrics (time, speed, smoothness, path) correlate with FMA-UE and have moderate-to-excellent test-retest ICCs. For CTS, one tablet spiral-drawing study with an SVM classifier reached sensitivity 82% and specificity 71% (AUC around 0.81). I found no tracing or spiral validation for CIPN.

### Cited Findings
**Spiral drawing, general and tremor (method validity)**
- Scoping review of the digitized Archimedes spiral test: 120 studies, 84 on PD or ET. Digital tablets were the most common device (66.6%). Only 52 studies (43.3%) reported measurement properties: 15 reliability, 45 validity, 6 responsiveness. The authors note that measurement properties outside tremor are underexplored and that digitization does not guarantee better reliability or validity. Wang S, Schwirtlich T, McLaughlin D, et al. (2025). *Clinical Applications and Measurement Properties of the Digitized Archimedes Spiral Drawing Test: A Scoping Review.* Mov Disord Clin Pract 12(11):1742–1755. DOI 10.1002/mdc3.70278 — [PMC12625189](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12625189/)
- Essential tremor: tablet log tremor amplitude correlated with the Fahn–Tolosa–Marín spiral rating at r = 0.94, with tablet test-retest ICC 0.97. Tablet and visual ratings had comparable minimum detectable change. *Digitizing Tablet and Fahn–Tolosa–Marín Ratings of Archimedes Spirals have Comparable Minimum Detectable Change in Essential Tremor.* Tremor Other Hyperkinet Mov. DOI 10.5334/tohm.344 — [tremorjournal.org](https://tremorjournal.org/articles/10.5334/tohm.344) (numbers from a search summary; full text not fetched)
- Computerized spiral scores correlated significantly with visual ratings in ET, and digital spiral analysis is proposed as a clinical trial outcome. Haubenberger D, et al. (2014). *Validation of Digital Spiral Analysis as Outcome Parameter for Clinical Trials in Essential Tremor.* Mov Disord. — [PMC4117681](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4117681/)
- Limitation: when tremor is severe and the patient cannot hold the pen, tremor cannot be assessed. *New insight in spiral drawing analysis methods – Application to action tremor quantification.* — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/318465679_New_insight_in_spiral_drawing_analysis_methods_-_Application_to_action_tremor_quantification)
- Vascular (subcortical) cognitive impairment: digitized straight-line drawing plus guided and free spirals, with pen pressure, speed and deviation from the target line compared in 24 patients vs 26 healthy controls. The abstract gives no r or AUC. Kim HJ, et al. (2025). *Kinematic characteristics in patients with subcortical vascular cognitive impairment: a quantitative analysis of digitized spiral drawing metrics.* Sci Rep. DOI 10.1038/s41598-025-88604-1 — [doi.org](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-88604-1)

**Stroke (tablet/touchscreen kinematics)**
- Touchscreen kinematic assessment after stroke, n = 64. Test-retest ICCs were moderate/good to excellent. All kinematic parameters **except trajectory length** showed convergent validity with FMA-UE, with significant correlations also reported against BBT and 9HPT. The tool is described as sensitive to the level of motor impairment. Exact r values were not in the abstract, and the full text was blocked. Goizueta S, Navarro MD, Calvo G, et al. (2025). *Touchscreen-based assessment of upper limb kinematics after stroke: Reliability, validity and sensitivity to motor impairment.* J NeuroEng Rehabil 22:27. DOI 10.1186/s12984-025-01563-6 — [PMC11817959](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11817959/)
- Tablet visually guided reaching in 20 chronic stroke, 20 age-matched and 20 young controls. Tablet metrics correlated strongly with Kinarm robot analyses, and correlations of tablet metrics with FMA were similar to the robot's. ICCs ranged from moderate to excellent. Ceiling effect: many stroke participants scored 66/66 on FMA. Austin DS, Dixon MJ, Tulimieri DT, et al. (2023). *Validating the measurement of upper limb sensorimotor behavior utilizing a tablet in neurologically intact controls and individuals with chronic stroke.* J NeuroEng Rehabil 20:114. DOI 10.1186/s12984-023-01240-6 — [JNER](https://jneuroengrehab.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12984-023-01240-6)
- Smoothness metrics during stroke recovery: the **jerk** metric correlated most strongly with change in FMA (r = 0.48), followed by speed (0.40), mean arrest period ratio (0.33), peaks (0.12) and tent (0.03). Rohrer B, Fasoli S, Krebs HI, et al. (2002). *Movement Smoothness Changes during Stroke Recovery.* J Neurosci 22(18):8297. DOI 10.1523/JNEUROSCI.22-18-08297.2002 — [jneurosci.org](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/jneuro/22/18/8297.full.pdf) (pre-2015, robot not tablet; included because it is the key reference for which smoothness metric to use)
- FMA scores relate to reaching kinematics in chronic hemiparesis (n = 34, optoelectronic system). A trunk anterior displacement cutoff of 9.0 cm separated mild/moderate from severe FMA impairment. Rech KD, et al. (2020). *Fugl-Meyer Assessment Scores Are Related With Kinematic Measures in People with Chronic Hemiparesis after Stroke.* J Stroke Cerebrovasc Dis. DOI 10.1016/j.jstrokecerebrovasdis.2019.104463 — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1052305719305464)

**Carpal tunnel syndrome**
- Tablet stylus **spiral drawing** with an SVM classifier, 33 CTS vs 31 healthy: sensitivity 82%, specificity 71%. A search summary gives AUC 0.81; the abstract fetched via Europe PMC did not report an AUC. Watanabe T, Koyama T, Yamada E, Fujita K, et al. (2021). *The Accuracy of a Screening System for Carpal Tunnel Syndrome Using Hand Drawing.* J Clin Med 10(19):4437. DOI 10.3390/jcm10194437 — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-0383/10/19/4437)

### Inferences
- Strongest metrics to compute from a trace: completion time, mean and SD of deviation from the template path (RMS error), mean speed, smoothness (normalized or log-dimensionless jerk; jerk is better than peak counting per Rohrer 2002), number of velocity peaks or pauses, and a tremor spectrum (power at 4–12 Hz via FFT of the radial deviation). A tremor spectrum from mouse input is likely degraded because mouse sampling and the mouse's mass filter tremor. It is more meaningful on touch or stylus.
- Because FMA has a ceiling in mildly impaired patients (Austin 2023), continuous kinematic metrics may be more sensitive for the at-home, post-discharge (mostly milder) population. This is plausible, but I found no head-to-head data.

### Gaps
- I found no study reporting r values between a **tracing/spiral** task and FMA-UE, ARAT or BBT in stroke. The exact r values in Goizueta 2025 could not be retrieved because the full text was blocked.
- No tracing or spiral validation in CIPN, and none after carpal tunnel release (as opposed to CTS diagnosis). No correlations with the Boston CTQ were found.
- Responsiveness (sensitivity to change) data for digital spirals are scarce: only 6 of 120 studies (Wang 2025).

## 2. Finger tapping and reaction time apps in stroke, CTS and CIPN

### Takeaway
In **CIPN**, the only app evidence is the NeuroDetect program from the University of Michigan (two small studies). Smartphone two-button finger tapping was the top contributor to hand-CIPN detection (85% of variable importance), but the hand model was not significant (AUC 0.679, p = .18). The feet model, using balance and gait, reached AUC 0.838. In **CTS**, thumb-movement and touchscreen apps from Fujita's group report sensitivity 82–94% and specificity 67–73% for screening. In stroke, gamified tapping is validated only in healthy adults so far. I found no validated reaction-time app for these three conditions.

### Cited Findings
**CIPN**
- NeuroDetect cross-sectional pilot, n = 26 (17 CIPN, 9 controls, by self-report). Tasks: a virtual 9-hole peg test (4 repetitions, 60 hand features) and a gait/balance test (87 features). Dominant-hand peg movement distance (mean P = .008, max P = .001, SD P < .001) and peg-removal speed (max P = .02, SD P = .01) were lower in cases. PLS-DA leave-one-out gave R² = 0.590 and RMSE = 0.312; no AUC was reported. Median CIPN20 was 28.1 in cases vs 10.0 in controls (P < .001). The authors call it a feasibility pilot that needs validation. Chen CS, Kim J, Garg N, ... Hertz DL (2021). *Chemotherapy-Induced Peripheral Neuropathy Detection via a Smartphone App: Cross-sectional Pilot Study.* JMIR mHealth uHealth 9(7):e27502. DOI 10.2196/27502 — [PMC8406129](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8406129/)
- NeuroDetect V2.0 prospective cohort, n = 45 with a neurological exam (24 CIPN-feet, 29 CIPN-hand). Finger tapping alternated index and middle fingers on two on-screen buttons, up to 100 taps or 100 s. The app-only model discriminated CIPN-f (AUC 83.8%, P = .02 vs no-information rate) but not CIPN-h (AUC 67.9%, P = .18). Finger tapping contributed 85% of variable importance to the CIPN-h model, and eyes-closed Romberg 40% to the CIPN-f model. App data plus CIPN20 was numerically, but not significantly, better than either alone. Finger tapping was more informative than the hole-peg test, though overall hand performance remained poor. The authors state that tapping is used in other neuropathies but has not been validated for CIPN. Chen CS, et al., Hertz DL (2025). *Remote Monitoring of Chemotherapy-Induced Peripheral Neuropathy by the NeuroDetect iOS App: Observational Cohort Study of Patients With Cancer.* J Med Internet Res 27:e65615. DOI 10.2196/65615 — [JMIR](https://www.jmir.org/2025/1/e65615) / [PMC11840369](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11840369/)
- Touch and manual action in CIPN (mixed methods, 2026): relevant to touch-based task design. Not fetched, so no numbers. — [Sci Rep](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-46039-2)

**CTS**
- Tablet app based on thumb movement (Fujita et al., 2019): 93% sensitivity and 73% specificity, from 29 CTS hands in 11 patients. Fujita K, et al. (2019). JMIR mHealth uHealth 7(9):e14172. DOI 10.2196/14172 — [JMIR](https://mhealth.jmir.org/2019/9/e14172/) (numbers from a search summary)
- Smartphone anomaly-detection screening (Koyama et al., 2021): 94% sensitivity, 67% specificity, AUC 0.86. — [PMC8005991](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8005991) (numbers from a search summary; full text not fetched)
- After **carpal tunnel release**: an RCT of touchscreen tablet exercises vs a paper exercise program found the tablet group improved more on the primary functional outcome. Secondary outcomes (grip, pain, dexterity) were not significant. Blanquero J, et al. (2019). *Exercises using a touchscreen tablet application improved functional ability more than an exercise program prescribed on paper in people after surgical carpal tunnel release: a randomised trial.* J Physiother. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1836955319300219) (this is an intervention, not an assessment)

**Tapping, other (method reliability)**
- Gamified tablet tapping test in 45 healthy adults: intra-rater ICC 0.80 (right) and 0.85 (left). Correlation with the conventional Tapping Test was r = 0.70 (right) and 0.42 (left). Correlations with BBT and 9HPT were weak. Moniz YF, Ferreira (2025). *Reliability of a gamified tablet-based tapping digital test for assessing motor coordination in adults.* Front Public Health. DOI 10.3389/fpubh.2025.1704140 — [doi.org](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpubh.2025.1704140)
- A smartphone tapping app was validated against conventional methods for bradykinesia in PD. *A Validation Study of a Smartphone-Based Finger Tapping Application for Quantitative Assessment of Bradykinesia in Parkinson's Disease.* — [PMC4965104](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4965104/)
- Open dataset of a smartphone finger tapping test, useful for ML baselines. *Dataset of Smartphone-Based Finger Tapping Test.* Sci Data (2024). DOI 10.1038/s41597-024-04052-y — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-024-04052-y)

### Inferences
- A tapping or reaction game is a reasonable second game for CIPN, since tapping is the top hand feature in NeuroDetect. Claims should still be framed as "monitoring trend," not "detection," given AUC 0.68 for hand CIPN.
- Sensory loss in CIPN and CTS (numbness) likely affects fine touch precision more than gross speed. Touch-phase features (inter-tap interval variability, touch duration, spatial scatter of taps around the target) are therefore candidate features. This is my inference, not tested in the sources.

### Gaps
- No validated reaction-time app for stroke upper limb, CTS or CIPN was found in this search.
- No study correlating app tapping with TNS, FACT/GOG-Ntx or the Boston CTQ was found. NeuroDetect used CIPN20 and a neurological exam.
- Stroke smartphone tapping validity vs FMA or ARAT: not found in this search (the Europe PMC query returned only Goizueta 2025).

## 3. Validated mouse-based (not touch) assessments

### Takeaway
I found **no validated mouse-based clinical assessment** for stroke, CTS or CIPN. Fitts' law evidence in stroke comes from arm reaching (robot or motion capture). Mouse-based Fitts tasks have been used in cerebral palsy. A mouse MVP is therefore a novel adaptation, not a validated instrument.

### Cited Findings
- Fitts' law in stroke, 20 stroke vs 10 controls in arm reaching: stroke patients had a higher Fitts slope and intercept, interpreted as greater neuromotor noise. McCrea PH, Eng JJ (2005). *Consequences of increased neuromotor noise for reaching movements in persons with stroke.* Exp Brain Res. DOI 10.1007/s00221-004-2048-1 — cited via [Frontiers Neurorobotics 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neurorobotics/articles/10.3389/fnbot.2021.610673/full)
- A normal-reaching model for evaluating post-stroke upper limb performance was compared against Fitts' law and a model originally built for **mouse pointing in cerebral palsy**, with a pilot of 12 post-stroke patients. — [PubMed 37226265](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37226265/)
- A Fitts reciprocal aiming task with an external optical mouse was used in cerebral palsy (not stroke). — [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT03002285](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03002285)
- Goal-oriented computer-based training in 98 stroke patients over 191 unsupervised sessions (planar reaching, not a mouse). Kinematic descriptors such as smoothness and work area, fed into regression, estimated FMA-UE (reported fit around 0.38, error 12.8 FMA points), CAHAI (around 0.40) and Barthel (around 0.35). The authors propose a "Total Goal-Directed Movement" digital biomarker. The statistic symbols did not render in the abstract, so these are likely R² or r values. Ballester BR, et al. (2021). *Estimating upper-extremity function from kinematics in stroke patients following goal-oriented computer-based training.* J NeuroEng Rehabil. DOI 10.1186/s12984-021-00971-8 — [PMC8720223](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8720223/)

### Inferences
- A mouse tests a different motor demand (wrist and finger on a supported surface, cursor gain) than touch reaching (shoulder and elbow). Validity from touchscreen reaching studies (Goizueta 2025, Austin 2023) does not transfer automatically.
- Mouse tasks may still suit CTS and CIPN (wrist and fine finger control), but nothing found tests this.

### Gaps
- No mouse study in stroke, CTS or CIPN with correlations to clinical scales was found.

## 4. Most predictive features and scoring approaches (incl. ML)

### Takeaway
Across studies, the features that carry signal are temporal (completion time, movement time, tap rate), variability (SD of speed, inter-tap interval, distance), smoothness (jerk, velocity peaks) and spatial accuracy (deviation from path). ML approaches have been simple and run on small samples: SVM (CTS drawing), PLS-DA and variable-importance models (CIPN), multivariate regression to clinical scores (stroke).

### Cited Findings
- CIPN: variability features (SD of peg movement distance, SD of peg-removal speed) were among the most discriminative, at VIP ≥ 2. Chen 2021 — [PMC8406129](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8406129/)
- CIPN: finger tapping accounted for 85% of variable importance in the hand model. Chen 2025 — [JMIR](https://www.jmir.org/2025/1/e65615)
- CTS: an SVM on stylus spiral-drawing kinematics gave sensitivity 82% and specificity 71%. Watanabe 2021 — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-0383/10/19/4437)
- Stroke: jerk-based smoothness is most correlated with FMA change (r = 0.48). Rohrer 2002 — [J Neurosci](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/jneuro/22/18/8297.full.pdf)
- Stroke: trajectory length was the only touchscreen kinematic **without** convergent validity with FMA-UE. Goizueta 2025 — [PMC11817959](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11817959/)
- Stroke: smoothness and work area regressed to FMA-UE. Ballester 2021 — [PMC8720223](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8720223/)
- Systematic review of smartphone hand function assessment (46 studies): the accelerometer was the most used sensor, then the camera. ML was mainly used for disease detection, severity and prediction. Fu Y, et al. (2024). *Smartphone-Based Hand Function Assessment: Systematic Review.* J Med Internet Res. DOI 10.2196/51564 — [doi.org](https://doi.org/10.2196/51564)
- Digital 9HPT reliability (sensorized board, healthy adults): test-retest ICC 0.81 (dominant) and 0.74 (non-dominant), concurrent validity with stopwatch ρ > 0.98. Biggio M, et al. (2026). *Automated Assessment of Manual Dexterity Using a Sensorized Nine-Hole Peg Test Board.* Sensors. DOI 10.3390/s26113497 — [doi.org](https://doi.org/10.3390/s26113497)

### Inferences
- Given small published samples, per-patient longitudinal scoring is more defensible for a hackathon than a population classifier. Options: z-scores against the patient's own baseline, or a reliable-change index using the session-to-session SD. Population classifiers (AUC 0.68–0.86) are not accurate enough for diagnosis.
- Suggested feature set per attempt: time, mean and RMS path error, % time off-path, mean and peak speed, log dimensionless jerk, number of velocity peaks, pause count, and tremor band power (touch or stylus only). For tapping: tap rate, inter-tap-interval CV, touch duration, spatial error, and fatigue slope (rate decline over the trial).

### Gaps
- No study reported which features best track **change over time** (responsiveness) in these conditions.

## 5. Best for a mouse-based MVP and "wow" factor

### Takeaway
For a mouse MVP, a **path/shape tracing game** (lines, then shapes, then an Archimedes spiral) plus a **Fitts-style target-reaching game** are the most defensible. Both borrow metrics validated in tablet and touchscreen stroke work and tremor work. A **two-target alternating tapping game** maps directly onto the strongest CIPN app feature, but is better on phone touch than with mouse clicks. The "wow" factor comes from showing live kinematics (speed and jerk heat-map overlaid on the trace, a tremor spectrum) and a personal progress trajectory against baseline.

### Cited Findings
- The spiral is the most-studied digital drawing test (120 studies), giving the team a literature anchor for metric definitions. Wang 2025 — [PMC12625189](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12625189/)
- Touchscreen target reaching after stroke is reliable and correlates with FMA-UE, BBT and 9HPT. Goizueta 2025 — [PMC11817959](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11817959/); Austin 2023 — [JNER](https://jneuroengrehab.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12984-023-01240-6)
- The NeuroDetect two-button alternating tap protocol (index and middle finger, 100 taps or 100 s) is a ready-made spec for the tapping game. Chen 2025 — [JMIR](https://www.jmir.org/2025/1/e65615)
- Spiral drawing plus ML for CTS shows the "draw a spiral, get a score" concept has precedent. Watanabe 2021 — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-0383/10/19/4437)

### Inferences
- Priority ranking for the MVP:
  1. Tracing (mouse works, rich metrics).
  2. Fitts reaching (mouse works, gives a clean "throughput" score in bits/s and a recognized index of difficulty).
  3. Tapping or reaction (defer to phone touch, since mouse clicks measure a different motor act).
- Position the product as **monitoring change against personal baseline**, not as a validated diagnostic. No validated mouse instrument exists, and condition-specific validation is limited to small pilots.

### Gaps
- No usability or adherence data were found for home-based gamified assessment in these three populations.
