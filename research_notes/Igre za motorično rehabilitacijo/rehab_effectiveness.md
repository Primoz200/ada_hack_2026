# Effectiveness of gamified / VR home rehabilitation and objective remote monitoring after discharge (stroke UL, carpal tunnel release, CIPN)

Context: ADAHACK 2026 (Ixtlan challenge). Evidence to motivate games that objectively track motor progress or regression, instead of subjective 1-10 self-ratings.
Method note: Abstracts were checked through Europe PMC, Cochrane.org and journal or index pages. Several PMC and PubMed full texts were blocked by captcha, so some figures come from abstracts or search snippets. Those cases are flagged.

## Q1. Effect sizes of VR / serious-game rehab vs conventional therapy for stroke upper limb (UL)

### Takeaway
The latest Cochrane review (Laver et al., 2025; 190 RCTs, 7,188 participants) finds that VR and interactive gaming give a small benefit over equal-dose conventional therapy for UL function (SMD 0.20, low certainty). The benefit is moderate when VR is added on top of usual care, which raises total therapy dose (SMD 0.42, moderate certainty). A meta-analysis restricted to serious games (Doumas 2021, 42 RCTs) found SMD 0.47. Home telerehab with game-like exercises matched in-clinic therapy on Fugl-Meyer (Cramer 2019). Main message for the pitch: games mainly work by adding dose and repetitions, and dose is exactly what an engaging home game can add.

### Cited Findings
- **Cochrane 2025 update.** Laver KE, Lange B, George S, Deutsch JE, Saposnik G, Chapman M, Crotty M. *Virtual reality for stroke rehabilitation.* Cochrane Database Syst Rev 2025;6:CD008349. DOI 10.1002/14651858.CD008349.pub5. Search to Sept 2023, 190 trials, 7,188 participants, 119 newly included. Only 36 trials (19%) had more than 50 participants, and the largest had 152. — [Europe PMC abstract, PMID 40537150](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/40537150); [Cochrane summary](https://www.cochrane.org/CD008349/STROKE_virtual-reality-for-stroke-rehabilitation)
  - VR vs alternative therapy, UL function and activity: **SMD 0.20 (95% CI 0.12–0.28); 67 studies, 2,830 participants; low certainty.** — [same](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/40537150)
  - VR added to usual care (more therapy time), UL function: **SMD 0.42 (0.26–0.58); 21 studies, 689 participants; moderate certainty.** — [same](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/40537150)
  - Balance: SMD 0.26 (0.12–0.40; 24 studies, 871 participants) vs alternative therapy, and SMD 0.68 (0.46–0.91; 12 studies, 321 participants) when added to usual care. Both are low certainty. — [same](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/40537150)
  - Activity limitation (ADL): SMD 0.21 (0.11–0.32; 33 studies, 1,495 participants; moderate certainty). Participation and QoL: SMD 0.11 (−0.02 to 0.24), no clear effect. — [same](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/40537150)
  - Safety: 59 studies monitored adverse events. Events were few and relatively mild, such as pain, headache and dizziness. — [same](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/40537150); [Cochrane summary](https://www.cochrane.org/CD008349/STROKE_virtual-reality-for-stroke-rehabilitation)
- **Cochrane 2017 version, for history.** Laver et al. 2017, DOI 10.1002/14651858.CD008349.pub4, 72 studies, 2,470 participants. VR vs conventional for UL: SMD 0.07 (−0.05 to 0.20; 22 studies, 1,038 participants; low quality), not significant. VR added to usual care: SMD 0.49 (0.21–0.77; 10 studies, 210 participants). ADL: SMD 0.25 (0.06–0.43; 10 studies, 466 participants). — [Cochrane Library pub4](https://cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD008349.pub4/information). Note: the 2017 equal-dose result was null. The 2025 update, with about 2.6x more trials, now shows a small significant benefit. Cite pub5 as current.
- **Serious-games meta-analysis.** Doumas I, Everard G, Dehem S, Lejeune T. *Serious games for upper limb rehabilitation after stroke: a meta-analysis.* J NeuroEng Rehabil 2021;18:100. DOI 10.1186/s12984-021-00889-1. 42 RCTs (PEDro ≥5), 1,760 participants. Results by ICF level: UL function **SMD 0.47 (95% CI 0.24–0.70)**, activity SMD 0.25, participation SMD 0.66. Retention at long-term follow-up for UL function: SMD 0.42. Games that complied with at least 8 of 11 neurorehabilitation principles had larger effects, regardless of device. The background states that about two thirds of stroke survivors keep UL impairment and few fully recover by 6 months. — [Europe PMC abstract](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/34130713); [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8204490/)
- **Home telerehab RCT (Fugl-Meyer).** Cramer SC et al. *Efficacy of Home-Based Telerehabilitation vs In-Clinic Therapy for Adults After Stroke: A Randomized Clinical Trial.* JAMA Neurol 2019;76(9):1079–1087. DOI 10.1001/jamaneurol.2019.1604. 124 patients, 4–36 weeks post-stroke, 11 US sites, 36 sessions of 70 min each, dose-matched. The home system used games and exercises. FM-UE gain was 7.86 (SD 6.68) for telerehab vs 8.36 (7.04) in clinic. Covariate-adjusted difference 0.06 (95% CI −2.14 to 2.26), so telerehab was **noninferior** (margin 2.47). Session adherence was **98.3% at home vs 93.3% in clinic**. Noninferiority was not shown for the SIS hand domain. — [PubMed 31233135](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31233135/); [PMC6593624](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6593624)

### Inferences
- The consistent finding is that gaming added to usual care (SMD about 0.42–0.49) beats gaming as a substitute (SMD about 0.07–0.20). For a home product this means games should add practice volume after discharge, not replace the therapist.
- An SMD of about 0.4 is a "small-to-moderate" effect. Doumas' subgroup suggests that design quality matters: games built on neurorehab principles such as repetition, task-specificity, progressive difficulty and feedback do better.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve a pooled Fugl-Meyer-specific MD (points) from the Cochrane 2025 review. The review pools SMDs across UL scales.
- Few trials in the pool are home-based or unsupervised. The Cochrane abstract does not split home vs clinic.

## Q2. Do gamification / games improve adherence and engagement?

### Takeaway
Evidence is suggestive but not conclusive. A cross-disciplinary review finds a positive effect of gamification on adherence. High adherence is reported in home telerehab with games (98% in Cramer 2019). But the evidence shows a wide range of adherence rates for unsupervised home gaming. No rehab-specific meta-analysis pools adherence.

### Cited Findings
- Home telerehab adherence was 98.3% vs 93.3% for in-clinic therapy. Adherence was objectively logged by the system. — [Cramer et al. 2019, JAMA Neurol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31233135/)
- A systematic review across health disciplines of 27 papers by De Croon et al. reported an overall positive effect of gamification on adherence. — [Frontiers in Psychology 2023, DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1155569 (as summarised in search; details not fully verified)](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1155569)
- Gamified mHealth in cardiovascular patients, meta-analysis of 5 trials: physical activity rose with Hedges g = 0.32 short term and g = 0.20 at about 2.5-month follow-up. "Feedback" was the most influential game element, followed by "avatar". — [PMC12070003 (search-snippet level)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12070003)
- An RCT of a 12-week unsupervised home game-based UL program after stroke (Home Virtual Rehabilitation System) took adherence from system timestamps. It notes that the literature reports "a range of adherence rates" to gamified home rehab, so gamification alone does not guarantee adherence. — [PMC11177982](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11177982/)
- A 2023 systematic review found "promising evidence" that unsupervised commercial exergames at home can complement rehabilitation. Adherence was a secondary outcome. — [search result, lirias.kuleuven.be/retrieve/610140](https://lirias.kuleuven.be/retrieve/610140)

### Inferences
- Objective, timestamped game logs are themselves a major advantage. They provide a verified adherence measure, which self-report cannot (see Q6, Nicolson 2018).
- Feedback, progression and personalisation appear to be the active ingredients for engagement.

### Gaps
- No meta-analysis specifically quantifies the adherence effect of gamified vs non-gamified home exercise in stroke, hand surgery or CIPN.

## Q3. Telerehab / app-based home exercise after carpal tunnel release (CTR)

### Takeaway
The evidence is small but positive. A 50-patient RCT found that touchscreen-tablet exercises improved QuickDASH by about 21 points more than a paper program at 4 weeks. An Australian pilot RCT found app-based care feasible and cheaper. A tablet thumb-movement game plus ML (SVM) could already discriminate CTS hands, which is directly relevant as proof-of-concept for game-based objective measurement.

### Cited Findings
- **Tablet exercise RCT (J Physiother 2019).** 50 people after surgical CTR. The tablet-app exercise group improved QuickDASH more than the paper-prescribed program at 4 weeks: **MD −21 points (95% CI −33 to −9)**. Secondary outcomes (grip strength, pain, dexterity) were not significantly different. Citation as indexed: Blanquero J et al., J Physiother 2019;65(2):81–87, DOI 10.1016/j.jphys.2019.02.008. Effect figures come from the DOAJ abstract record; author and DOI should be verified. — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/1f302258f47c48398a203b73c604bfcc)
- **App-based home program after CTR (Metro North, Queensland pilot RCT).** A "technology-enhanced group-based model of care" was compared with a written home program and in-person follow-up. Acceptability, feasibility, attendance and home-program adherence were high in both groups. Cost was about AUD 68 per participant vs AUD 110 for usual care. The authors call for a larger RCT. — [Metro North research snapshot 2024](https://metronorth.health.qld.gov.au/researchsnapshot2024/tech-model-care-ctr)
- **Preoperative telerehab (2023 RCT, n=30).** Adding pain neuroscience education to telerehab exercise improved kinesiophobia and symptom severity in the short term. There was no between-group difference in pain intensity. — [PubMed 37572618](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37572618/)
- A guideline cited in an RCT of home therapy after endoscopic release (50 home therapy vs 55 no therapy) found **no high-quality evidence** comparing home therapy with no therapy after CTR. — [PMC10786107](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10786107)
- **Game-plus-ML proof of concept.** Fujita K et al. *A Tablet-Based App for Carpal Tunnel Syndrome Screening: Diagnostic Case-Control Study.* JMIR mHealth uHealth 2019;7(9):e14172. DOI 10.2196/14172. An iPad game recorded thumb-movement speed and timing in 29 CTS hands vs 11 controls. CTS hands moved significantly slower in the 3 and 6 o'clock directions (p=.03, p=.005) and took longer to reach targets in most directions. An SVM classifier (leave-one-out) was used for real-time screening. — [Europe PMC, PMID 31586365](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/31586365)
- An RCT of telerehabilitation in CTS is registered. — [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT05184413](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05184413)

### Inferences
- The CTR evidence base is thin: small, single-centre trials with short follow-up. A hackathon pitch can frame this as an evidence gap that objective game data could help fill.
- The Fujita study shows that short game sessions produce kinematic features that separate impaired from normal hands. That is the same pipeline the team proposes for tracking recovery or regression after CTR.

### Gaps
- I found no meta-analysis of telerehab after CTR, and no trial of gamified exercise specifically after CTR with objective tracking of recovery trajectory.

## Q4. Exercise / sensorimotor / balance training for CIPN — effect sizes

### Takeaway
Exercise reduces CIPN symptoms with a moderate effect (SMD −0.62 in a 15-RCT meta-analysis) and improves balance. The strongest single trial (STOP, JAMA Intern Med 2024) showed that sensorimotor training during chemotherapy cut CIPN incidence from 70.6% to 30.0%. Sensorimotor and balance tasks are therefore an evidence-based target for exergames.

### Cited Findings
- **Huang Y et al.** *Exercise for reducing chemotherapy-induced peripheral neuropathy: a systematic review and meta-analysis of RCTs.* Front Neurol 2023;14:1252259. DOI 10.3389/fneur.2023.1252259. 15 RCTs, 1,124 patients. Total symptom score **SMD −0.62 (95% CI −0.99 to −0.24)**. Exercise also improved numbness, tingling, QoL, pain, **balance** and FACT/GOG-NTX. The protocols were heterogeneous. — [Europe PMC, PMID 38283674](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/38283674)
- **Lin WL et al.** *The effects of exercise on CIPN symptoms in cancer patients: SR and MA.* Support Care Cancer 2021;29:5303–5311. DOI 10.1007/s00520-021-06082-3. 5 studies, 178 participants, hospital or home. Pooled MD 0.53 (95% CI 0.23–0.83; p=.0006). Sensorimotor exercise reduced CIPN-related loss of postural stability. — [Europe PMC, PMID 33660078](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/33660078)
- **Streckmann F et al.** *Preventive Effect of Neuromuscular Training on Chemotherapy-Induced Neuropathy: A Randomized Clinical Trial (STOP).* JAMA Intern Med 2024;184(9):1046–1053. DOI 10.1001/jamainternmed.2024.2354. Design: 158 randomized patients on oxaliplatin or vinca alkaloids, 4 centres near Cologne, with sensorimotor training (SMT, n=55), whole-body vibration (WBV, n=53) or usual care (n=50). Sessions were supervised, twice weekly, 15–30 min. **CIPN incidence was 30.0% for SMT (12/40) and 41.2% for WBV (14/34) vs 70.6% for usual care (24/34); p=.002.** The vinca-alkaloid plus SMT subgroup benefited most, and effects were larger per-protocol. Note that the analysed denominators are smaller than the randomized numbers. — [Europe PMC, PMID 38949824](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/38949824)
- A 2024 meta-analysis (20 studies, 1,308 participants) found a small significant improvement, with exercise frequency moderating the effect. Another pooled analysis reported Hedges g = 0.28 (HK-adjusted 95% CI 0.12–0.45) with a prediction interval of about −0.1 to 0.66. — [Frontiers in Neurology 2024, DOI 10.3389/fneur.2024.1346099 (snippet-level; verify which paper reports which figure)](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fneur.2024.1346099/pdf)
- Duregon F et al. *Effects of exercise on cancer patients suffering CIPN undergoing treatment: a systematic review.* Crit Rev Oncol Hematol 2018;121:90–100. DOI 10.1016/j.critrevonc.2017.11.002. Combined endurance, strength and sensorimotor protocols showed larger improvements. — [PubMed 29198853](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29198853/)
- Kanzawa-Lee GA et al. *Exercise Effects on CIPN: A Comprehensive Integrative Review.* Cancer Nurs 2020. DOI 10.1097/NCC.0000000000000801. It found no high-quality evidence at the time, which tempers the positive findings above. — [PubMed 32187026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32187026/)

### Inferences
- Balance and sensorimotor training is the CIPN modality with the clearest mechanistic and trial support. A balance or fine-motor exergame, using phone IMU, camera or touch input, maps directly onto that evidence.
- STOP used supervised sessions. Whether unsupervised home gamified training reproduces the effect is untested.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve a pooled SMD specific to balance outcomes in CIPN; Huang 2023 reports balance improved but the number was not in the abstract.
- I found no RCT of exergames or VR specifically for CIPN within this budget.

## Q5. Does post-discharge remote monitoring reduce readmissions or catch complications earlier?

### Takeaway
The evidence is mixed but informative. Frequent structured monitoring catches problems that usual care misses. In PVC-RAM-1, drug errors were detected in 29.7% vs 5.5% of patients, and acute care use was non-significantly 20% lower. Routine symptom monitoring in oncology reduced ER visits and improved QoL, with an overall survival signal. Pooled readmission reductions are clearest in heart failure. In other settings the certainty of evidence is low or very low.

### Cited Findings
- **PVC-RAM-1 RCT.** McGillion MH et al. *Post-discharge after surgery Virtual Care with Remote Automated Monitoring-1 technology versus standard care: randomised controlled trial.* BMJ 2021;374:n2209. DOI 10.1136/bmj.n2209. 905 adults after non-elective surgery at 8 Canadian hospitals. The intervention ran 30 days, with daily vitals, wound photos and virtual nurse contact. — [Europe PMC, PMID 34593374](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/34593374)
  - Primary outcome, days alive at home: 29.7 vs 29.5 (RR 1.01, 0.99–1.02), with no difference.
  - Acute hospital care: 22.0% vs 27.3% (RR 0.80, 0.64–1.01), a borderline result.
  - **Drug error detected: 29.7% vs 5.5%** (absolute difference 24.2%). Drug error corrected: 28% vs 4%.
  - Fewer patients reported pain at days 7, 15 and 30 (absolute differences 13.9%, 11.9% and 9.6%). — [PMC8477638](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8477638); [McMaster news](https://news.mcmaster.ca/virtual-care-with-remote-monitoring-catches-drug-errors-and-reduces-patient-pain/)
- **Oncology symptom monitoring RCT.** Basch E et al. *Symptom Monitoring With Patient-Reported Outcomes During Routine Cancer Treatment: A Randomized Controlled Trial.* J Clin Oncol 2016;34(6):557–565. DOI 10.1200/JCO.2015.63.0830. 766 patients. HRQL improved in 34% vs 18% and worsened in 38% vs 53%. Fewer ER visits in the intervention arm (about 7% absolute, p=.02). — [Europe PMC, PMID 26644527](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/26644527); [DCRI slides](https://dcricollab.dcri.duke.edu/sites/NIHKR/KR/GR-Slides-03-17-23.pdf)
- **Overall survival follow-up.** Basch E et al. *Overall Survival Results of a Trial Assessing Patient-Reported Outcomes for Symptom Monitoring During Routine Cancer Treatment.* JAMA 2017;318(2):197–198. DOI 10.1001/jama.2017.7156. Median OS 31.2 vs 26.0 months; this analysis was not prespecified. — [MSKCC](https://www.mskcc.org/blog/asco17-online-symptom-monitoring-tool-improves-survival-those-undergoing-chemotherapy-metastatic); [UNC](https://sph.unc.edu/?p=294320)
- **Multicentre replication (Nature Medicine 2025, PRO-TECT).** Cumulative incidence of emergency visits fell by 6.1%, and mean visits at 12 months were 1.02 vs 1.30. Overall survival was similar. — [UNC news 2025](https://www.unc.edu/posts/2025/03/05/home-based-monitoring-improves-quality-of-life/); [drugs.com](https://www.drugs.com/news/patient-reported-symptom-monitoring-aids-cancer-outcomes-123734.html)
- **Virtual wards after discharge.** A meta-analysis of 24 RCTs (10,876 patients) found fewer deaths and readmissions in heart failure. Results for other conditions were less consistent. — [PMC9240908 / PubMed 35763296](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35763296/)
- **Remote vital-signs monitoring of acutely ill patients at home** (JMIR 2025 SR). Certainty of evidence for readmission and mortality was very low. — [JMIR 2025;e64753](https://jmir.org/2025/1/e64753)

### Inferences
- The strongest argument is detection plus action. Monitoring helps when abnormal signals trigger clinician escalation, as with nurse alerts in Basch and RAM escalation in PVC-RAM. The product should therefore pair objective game metrics with alert thresholds and a clinician workflow.
- Note: Basch's monitoring was based on subjective PROs. It shows that frequent structured monitoring helps. It does not show that objective beats subjective measurement.

### Gaps
- I found no RCT that directly compares objective sensor or game-based motor monitoring with self-report for detecting post-discharge deterioration in stroke, CTR or CIPN. This is a genuine gap and the team's opportunity.

## Q6. Limitations of subjective numeric rating scales and self-report

### Takeaway
Self-report is systematically biased and unreliable for tracking change. Self-reported exercise adherence was overestimated, and a 0–10 self-rated adherence scale had limited validity and less-than-acceptable test-retest reliability against hidden accelerometers. Different people use the 0–10 scale differently. NRS data are ordinal, not interval. Recall worsens with longer recall periods. Self-reported activity is overestimated against devices across many rehab populations.

### Cited Findings
- **Concealed accelerometer study.** Nicolson PJA, Hinman RS, Wrigley TV, Stratford PW, Bennell KL. *Self-reported Home Exercise Adherence: A Validity and Reliability Study Using Concealed Accelerometers.* J Orthop Sports Phys Ther 2018;48(12):943–950. DOI 10.2519/jospt.2018.8275. 54 adults on a 12-week home program. Diaries overestimated exercises (median 220 vs 176 accelerometer-measured). An 11-point (0–10) self-rated adherence NRS had "limited validity and less than acceptable test-retest reliability". — [Europe PMC, PMID 30053792](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/30053792)
- In low back pain, self-report overestimated moderate activity by 42 min/day and vigorous activity by 39 min/day, and underestimated sedentary time by 122 min/day vs accelerometry. — [PMC4889825](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4889825/)
- In coronary artery disease, self-report overestimated MVPA by 108 min/day and underestimated sedentary behaviour by 174 min/day. — [PMC8464699](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8464699/)
- After bariatric surgery, self-report vs accelerometer correlations were poor (r = 0.21–0.42) and worsened over time. — [PMC7045736](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7045736/)
- **Inter-individual scale use.** Pain rating schemas vary: one cluster mainly uses the upper part of the 0–10 scale and another the lower part. Midpoints are not operationally defined. — [Journal of Pain Research (Dovepress), "Pain rating schema: three distinct subgroups"](https://www.dovepress.com/pain-rating-schema-three-distinct-subgroups-of-individuals-emerge-when-peer-reviewed-fulltext-article-JPR)
- **Ordinal, not interval.** An IRT analysis of postoperative pain NRS concluded it should be used as ordinal, not interval. — [Frontiers in Pain Research 2024, DOI 10.3389/fpain.2024.1415635](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/pain-research/articles/10.3389/fpain.2024.1415635/pdf)
- **Measurement error and recall.** The minimal important difference of the pain NRS is about 2 points, so changes of 1 point are within noise. Test-retest reliability for NRS pain in limb musculoskeletal patients is "very good to excellent" in a systematic review, which counterbalances the above. — [PMC5014406](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5014406). Current state influences recall, and 1-week pain recall in rheumatoid arthritis was worse than 1-day recall. — [PMC10336923 (snippet-level)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10336923)
- **Capacity vs perceived use mismatch after stroke.** 18% of patients at 6 months and 23% at 12 months had good observed arm function but low self-perceived arm use. — [Neurol Res Pract 2021, DOI 10.1186/s42466-021-00164-7](https://link.springer.com/10.1186/s42466-021-00164-7)

### Inferences
- With a minimal important difference of about 2/10, idiosyncratic scale use and ordinal properties, a daily 1–10 rating cannot reliably detect small gradual motor changes. Continuous game kinematics such as reaction time, movement speed, accuracy and smoothness can.
- The Nicolson result is the cleanest single citation: a 0–10 self-rating of adherence was invalid and unreliable compared with an objective sensor.

### Gaps
- I found no source quantifying terminal-digit preference or heaping, such as rounding to 0, 5 or 10, in 0–10 NRS. Searches returned nothing specific. Ceiling effects of NRS in rehab were not specifically sourced.

## Q7. Time course of motor change after stroke (justifying frequent monitoring)

### Takeaway
Most spontaneous UL recovery happens in the first 6–10 weeks and follows a roughly logarithmic curve. Many patients recover about 70% of their initial impairment by 3–6 months, but about 30% of severely impaired patients are "non-recoverers". Trajectories are individual, and some patients plateau or decline in the chronic phase. The proportional recovery rule cannot model recovery rate, so repeated measurements are needed to update predictions. All of this supports dense, frequent, objective measurement after discharge.

### Cited Findings
- Kwakkel et al.: in 101 first-ever MCA ischaemic stroke patients, time alone explained 16–42% of improvements in the first 6–10 weeks, reflecting spontaneous recovery. — [research.vu.nl PDF](https://research.vu.nl/ws/files/2237827/196230.pdf)
- Buma, Kwakkel, Ramsey: recovery follows a natural logarithmic pattern, and intensive task-oriented practice can modify it, preferably within 6 months. Recovery mechanisms operate in limited time windows. — [Repub EUR / search snippet](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/124306)
- Proportional recovery rule: recovery in non-severe UL impairment over 3–6 months is about 0.7 times the initial FM-UE impairment. About 30% of severe patients do not follow the rule. The rule "does not model the time course of recovery early poststroke… cannot model the rate of recovery nor update predictions with repeated measurements in time". — [PMC7065018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7065018); [van der Vliet et al., mixture model, PubMed 31925838](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31925838/)
- Winters et al.: about 70% of 146 patients showed fixed proportional UL recovery of about 78%. Patients unlikely to reach the predicted recovery could be identified by clinical determinants. — [Amsterdam UMC PDF](https://pure.amsterdamumc.nl/ws/files/160745082/Generalizability-of-the-proportional-recovery-model-for-the-upper-extremity-after-an-ischemic-stroke.pdf)
- After discharge from inpatient rehab (n=176), FM-UE continued to improve by about 5.0 (3.7–6.4) points per year at the group level. — [PMC5470733 / snippet](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5470733)
- In mild to moderate impairment, UL use plateaued and "a small proportion decreased" in the early chronic phase. — [search-snippet level; source among PMC9989222 / Clin Rehabil 2022, DOI 10.1177/02692155221134413](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/02692155221134413)
- About two thirds of survivors keep UL impairment, and few attain complete recovery at 6 months. — [Doumas 2021](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/34130713)

### Inferences
- Because the steepest change happens in weeks 0–10, which overlaps the post-discharge period, weekly or monthly clinic assessments undersample the trajectory. Daily game-derived scores would allow per-patient recovery curves, early flagging of patients deviating from expected (proportional) recovery, and detection of plateau or decline.
- Mixture or longitudinal ML models (van der Vliet) explicitly benefit from repeated measures, which supports the team's ML approach.

### Gaps
- I found no robust estimate of the proportion of patients whose UL function deteriorates after discharge, or of when this happens. Evidence on late decline is limited to "small proportion" statements.
- I found no direct evidence that daily vs weekly monitoring changes outcomes.
