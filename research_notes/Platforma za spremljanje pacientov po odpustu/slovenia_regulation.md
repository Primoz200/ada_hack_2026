## 1. Problem size in Slovenia (stroke, carpal tunnel, CIPN, workforce, waiting lists, ageing)

### Takeaway
Solid, dated numbers exist for stroke (about 4,000 strokes a year; 5,274 cerebrovascular hospitalisations and 1,875 deaths in 2024; URI Soča can take only about 260 stroke patients a year), for primary-care and physiotherapy bottlenecks (134,737 people without a personal doctor on 31 March 2026; 37,873 people waiting too long for physiotherapy) and for ageing (22.4% aged 65+). No Slovenian national count was found for carpal tunnel release operations or for patients on neurotoxic chemotherapy; those must be presented as gaps, not numbers.

### Cited Findings

Stroke
- About 4,000 people a year have a stroke in Slovenia and almost 2,000 die (about 800 men, 1,200 women), according to Matija Cevc (Društvo za zdravje srca in ožilja Slovenije), reported 13 May 2025. — [RTV SLO/STA, 13 May 2025](https://www.rtvslo.si/dostopno/clanki/napovedi-glede-prihodnje-pojavnosti-mozganske-kapi-skrb-vzbujajoce/745070)
- Same press conference (Tatjana Erjavec, Združenje bolnikov s cerebrovaskularno boleznijo Slovenije): only about 20% of survivors have no lasting consequences; about 75% return home after treatment, where relatives carry the burden; roughly 10,000 more people in Slovenia are expected to live with permanent consequences by 2030; the organisers cited a projected 35% rise in stroke patients by 2040. — [RTV SLO/STA, 13 May 2025](https://www.rtvslo.si/dostopno/clanki/napovedi-glede-prihodnje-pojavnosti-mozganske-kapi-skrb-vzbujajoce/745070)
- NIJZ Zdravstveni statistični letopis 2024 (chapter 2.4.1, data year 2024): 5,274 hospitalisations for cerebrovascular diseases (MKB-10 I60–I69; men 2,641, women 2,633), i.e. 2.4 per 1,000 population; hospitalisation rate in the 75+ group 19.2 per 1,000 men and 17.1 per 1,000 women; 1,875 deaths (769 men, 1,106 women). Self-reported stroke among adults aged 25–74 was 1.7% in 2024 (survey). — [NIJZ, 2.4.1 SZB 2024 (published Dec 2025)](https://nijz.si/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/2.4.1_SZB_2024_Z.pdf)
- Note on the two stroke figures: "about 4,000 strokes" (expert statement) and "5,274 hospitalisations" (NIJZ) are not the same measure; the NIJZ figure covers all cerebrovascular diagnoses I60–I69 and counts hospitalisations, not first-ever strokes. — [RTV SLO](https://www.rtvslo.si/dostopno/clanki/napovedi-glede-prihodnje-pojavnosti-mozganske-kapi-skrb-vzbujajoce/745070); [NIJZ](https://nijz.si/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/2.4.1_SZB_2024_Z.pdf)

Rehabilitation pathway and capacity after stroke
- URI Soča authors (Goljar, Bizovičar; Rehabilitacija 2017) report that the institute's contract with ZZZS allows admission of up to 260 stroke patients a year, roughly one tenth of those who need rehabilitation after stroke; demand far exceeds capacity and regional distribution of rehabilitation places is uneven. — [Rehabilitacija 2017, suppl. 1, pp. 61–68](https://ibmi.mf.uni-lj.si/rehabilitacija/vsebina/Rehabilitacija_2017_S1_p061-068.pdf)
- Same paper: rehabilitation teams are often incomplete, there are no uniform treatment protocols across programmes, health and social services in the home community are described as scarce and poorly organised, relatives usually take over all care after discharge, and needs of patients and families are reviewed at least once a year after discharge. — [Rehabilitacija 2017, suppl. 1](https://ibmi.mf.uni-lj.si/rehabilitacija/vsebina/Rehabilitacija_2017_S1_p061-068.pdf)
- URI Soča review (Bizovičar, 2023) on telerehabilitation for upper-limb function after stroke: hospital stays after stroke are getting shorter, care is shifting to outpatient settings and discharge does not always coincide with adequate functional recovery; telerehabilitation could extend care begun in rehabilitation units. — [Rehabilitacija 2023, no. 1, pp. 56–62](https://ibmi.mf.uni-lj.si/rehabilitacija/vsebina/Rehabilitacija_2023_No1_p56-62.pdf)
- ESO guidance as summarised by Goljar (2010): rehabilitation should continue through the first year after stroke; early supported discharge with home rehabilitation is recommended for mild-to-moderate deficits, with a home team of at least nurse, physiotherapist and occupational therapist. — [Rehabilitacija 2010, suppl. 1](https://dlib.si/stream/URN:NBN:SI:DOC-L5M3I7XV/be983557-6c9b-4df1-951c-8cb51305cfb2/PDF)
- Same review: the move from hospital to home is described as always a critical period; continuous rehabilitation in the first year after discharge lowers the risk of functional decline; poor information for patients and families predicts worse quality of life, and formal caregiver training lowers costs and improves patients' quality of life. — [Rehabilitacija 2010, suppl. 1, pp. 121–127](https://ibmi.mf.uni-lj.si/rehabilitacija/vsebina/Rehabilitacija_2010_S1_p121-127.pdf)
- Post-acute options in Slovenia as listed by a regional hospital team (SB Nova Gorica, 2012): rehabilitation in health resorts (*zdravilišča*), prolonged hospital care units, nursing homes (DSO), or direct discharge home with later referral to URI Soča for more demanding programmes once the patient can take part. In their 21-patient series, rehabilitation started on average 15.9 days after the stroke and lasted about 38 days; 81% were discharged home and 19% needed a nursing home; only 14 of 21 came to a check-up, held on average 121 days after discharge; the authors found no comparable outcome studies from other Slovenian post-acute stroke units. — [Černe et al., Rehabilitacija 2012; XI(1)](https://dlib.si/stream/URN:NBN:SI:DOC-ZFM11DGZ/fe12cfb7-0334-4196-b19d-b8d8b5a60c03/PDF)
- URI Soča home telerehabilitation pilot (Goljar et al., 2016): rehabilitation programmes in Slovenia last at best up to three months; after inpatient rehabilitation selected patients (FIM 40–80, spastic non-functional upper limb with Modified Ashworth ≥2, and a carer willing to help) got three months of home telerehabilitation with a weekly video call from an occupational therapist and a physiotherapist, using tablets with mobile internet; relatives were trained in the last week before discharge and were needed especially at the start. — [Rehabilitacija 2016; XV(3): 63–69](https://dlib.si/stream/URN:NBN:SI:DOC-6VE255PI/ad0ea288-fb09-4500-8687-b9c58d0cfffe/PDF)
- Spa (health-resort) treatment can start straight after hospital treatment: the treating hospital doctor proposes it and ZZZS decides. — [ZZZS poster "Specialistično bolnišnična dejavnost", Jan 2025](https://api.zzzs.si/ZZZS/info/egradiva.nsf/0/3cdf2381d29fef48c12585230041239f/$FILE/plakat%20Specialisti%C4%8Dno%20bolni%C5%A1ni%C4%8Dna%20dejavnost%20januar%202025.pdf)
- Acute phase: the TeleKap telestroke network links 12 hospitals with about 100 consultations a month. — [NIJZ eZdravje deck (uploaded May 2026)](https://nijz.si/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Digitalizacija-zdravstva-in-storitve-eZdravja-v-Sloveniji-Hajdi-Kosednar.pdf)

Physiotherapy bottleneck
- ZZZS data reported by 24ur (article undated in the extraction; internal references point to 2025): 2,107 physiotherapists including concessionaires (1,179 in 2015); patients waiting beyond the permitted time for physiotherapy rose from 8,136 five years earlier to 37,873 (+465%); waiting times roughly doubled at all urgency levels; annual cost of primary-care physiotherapy rose from just over EUR 20 million to about EUR 38 million. Example ZD Škofja Loka: average wait for a first physiotherapy visit 243.9 days ("hitro") and 305.8 days ("redno"). — [24ur "Dejstva" (date to verify)](https://www.24ur.com/novice/dejstva/fizioterapevtov-vec-vrsta-cakajocih-pa-vse-daljsa-kako-je-to-mogoce.html)
- Electronic physiotherapy work orders (*eDN FT*) became mandatory in September 2025. — [NIJZ eZdravje slide deck (uploaded May 2026)](https://nijz.si/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Digitalizacija-zdravstva-in-storitve-eZdravja-v-Sloveniji-Hajdi-Kosednar.pdf)

Family doctors
- On 31 March 2026, 134,737 insured persons (adults and children; 31,437 of them foreigners) had no chosen personal doctor (*izbrani osebni zdravnik*); in January 2026 the figure was 139,444, about 7% of insured persons; ZZZS estimates at least 200 more family-medicine teams are needed. — [Svet24 citing ZZZS, 15 Apr 2026](https://svet24.si/zdravje/134000-ljudi-brez-osebnega-zdravnika-1892975)
- Headline only (not fetched): 1,071 family doctors work in the public system out of 1,447 in total (Zdravniška zbornica). — [RTV SLO (title/snippet only)](https://www.rtvslo.si/slovenija/zdravniska-zbornica-razmere-v-druzinski-medicini-terjajo-sistemsko-preobrazbo/746185)

Waiting lists (NIJZ eNaročanje reports)
- Latest fully verified NIJZ snapshot found is old: on 1 May 2023, 141,111 patients (50.26% of all waiting) were waiting beyond the permitted time; first examinations: 124,994 waiting, 70,785 over the limit. — [N1/STA, 28 May 2023](https://n1info.si/novice/slovenija/nijz-vsak-drugi-pacient-na-zdravstvene-storitve-caka-nad-dopustno-mejo/)
- On 1 November 2023, 74% of patients waiting for a first examination with urgency "zelo hitro" had waited beyond the permitted 14 days. — [N1/STA, 31 Dec 2023](https://n1info.si/novice/slovenija/cakalne-vrste-v-zdravstvu-so-se-spet-podaljsale/)
- The permitted wait for "zelo hitro" referrals was later extended from 14 to 30 days (mentioned in the 24ur physiotherapy article). — [24ur](https://www.24ur.com/novice/dejstva/fizioterapevtov-vec-vrsta-cakajocih-pa-vse-daljsa-kako-je-to-mogoce.html)
- Title only (not fetched): an STA item says a new rulebook statistically reduced the number of impermissibly long waiters, so pre- and post-change counts are not comparable. — [TaxFinLex/STA (title only)](https://www.tax-fin-lex.si/home/novica/30949)

Carpal tunnel syndrome (secondary scenario, kept brief)
- Slovenian recommendations proposal (Podnar, Zdravniški vestnik 2008): Slovenian data on the number of median-nerve release operations were explicitly not available; about half of patients with electrophysiologically confirmed peripheral nerve damage seen in the Ljubljana EMG laboratory had carpal tunnel syndrome; three years after a confirmed diagnosis only 52% had been operated on (citing Rigler & Podnar 2006). — [Zdrav Vestn 2008; 77: 103–9](https://www.dlib.si/stream/URN:NBN:SI:DOC-E5IAUE7K/f54412ca-02d5-4a8e-b2d3-1e6edd65bc08/PDF)
- Usual follow-up per the same paper: a surgeon check about two weeks after the operation for wound healing; if symptoms persist, conservative care via the personal doctor and repeat nerve-conduction studies no earlier than 3–6 months. No structured rehabilitation programme or outcome questionnaire is described. — [Zdrav Vestn 2008](https://www.dlib.si/stream/URN:NBN:SI:DOC-E5IAUE7K/f54412ca-02d5-4a8e-b2d3-1e6edd65bc08/PDF)
- Unverified headline: a Svet24 related-article teaser mentions "around 60,000 Slovenians" with carpal tunnel problems; the underlying article was not read. — [Svet24, 26 Jun 2026 (teaser only)](https://svet24.si/zdravje/karpalni-kanal-fizioterapija-operacija-1907884)

Oncology and CIPN (secondary scenario, kept brief)
- More than 19,000 people are diagnosed with cancer in Slovenia each year and more than 130,000 live with a cancer diagnosis (Onkološki inštitut Ljubljana, World Cancer Day statement). — [N1/STA, 4 Feb 2026](https://n1info.si/novice/slovenija/onkoloski-institut-pozval-k-sistemskim-pristopom-za-ucinkovito-obravnavo-bolnikov-z-rakom/)
- Review by Onkološki inštitut Ljubljana authors (Dejanović, Andlovic, Škrbinc; journal/year not shown in the PDF text, references run to 2022): main causes of CIPN are taxanes, platinum derivatives and vinca alkaloids; oxaliplatin causes acute transient neuropathy in 65–98% of patients; recovery after taxanes may take up to three years and some cases become chronic; diagnosis is clinical, with EORTC QLQ-CIPN20 named as the questionnaire and EMG as the objective method; duloxetine is first-line drug treatment; exercise for distal strength, coordination and balance is listed among non-drug measures. — [dLib PDF](https://dlib.si/stream/URN:NBN:SI:DOC-SY4JHY6K/a8136b0d-71e2-4359-a880-8b65c3112833/PDF)

Ageing
- Slovenia has more than 2.13 million inhabitants, average age 44.5 years, 22.4% aged 65 or more, and over 141 people aged 65+ per 100 children (SURS figures as reported; reference date not given in the article). — [RTV SLO, 5 Feb 2026](https://www.rtvslo.si/n/771340)

### Inferences
- The defensible "problem in numbers" slide: ~4,000 strokes/year, most survivors go home with deficits, specialised inpatient rehabilitation at URI Soča reaches roughly one in ten, community physiotherapy has tens of thousands waiting beyond the permitted time, and about 135,000 people have no personal doctor. Each of those has a dated source above.
- For carpal tunnel and CIPN the honest framing is "common, follow-up is thin (one surgical check at ~2 weeks; CIPN assessed clinically at visits), national counts are not published", rather than a specific number.
- The age gradient in NIJZ data (stroke hospitalisation almost entirely in 65+) combined with 22.4% aged 65+ supports the "demand will grow" argument without needing the 2030/2040 projections, which are advocacy-group statements.

### Gaps
- No Slovenian annual count of carpal tunnel release operations was found (the 2008 Zdravniški vestnik paper says the data were unavailable; nothing newer located). NIJZ/ZZZS procedure statistics were not queried directly.
- No count of patients receiving taxanes/platinum/vinca alkaloids at Onkološki inštitut Ljubljana and no Slovenian CIPN prevalence figure were found; OI annual-report numbers on systemic therapy were not retrieved.
- Current (2026) NIJZ national waiting-list totals were not retrieved; the verified snapshots are from 2023 and methodology changed since.
- No verified waiting time for admission to URI Soča or for health-resort (*zdraviliško*) rehabilitation was found. The role of *patronažna služba* after stroke discharge was not sourced.
- OECD/European Observatory Country Health Profile 2025 for Slovenia exists ([OECD page](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/2025/12/country-health-profile-2025-country-notes_7e72146d/slovenia_27d174ae.html)) but its body text could not be extracted; no figures taken from it.
- First-ever stroke incidence from a registry (as opposed to hospitalisations or expert estimates) was not found.
- National shares of stroke patients going to URI Soča, health resorts, home or nursing homes were not found; the only split located is the 21-patient Nova Gorica series (2012).

## 1b. Who does what after discharge in Slovenia: hospital, personal doctor, patronažna služba, relatives (stroke-weighted)

### Takeaway
On paper the hand-over is simple: the hospital sends an *odpustno pismo* to the *izbrani osebni zdravnik*, who then orders physiotherapy and community-nursing visits by *delovni nalog*. In practice Slovenian sources describe non-standardised and often missing discharge information for *patronažna služba*, community nurses stretched across about 2,600 inhabitants each, about 135,000 people with no personal doctor at all, and relatives carrying most of the care. That is the "lost insight" gap the sponsor's brief describes.

### Cited Findings

Hospital to personal doctor
- At discharge the hospital doctor sends the discharge letter (*odpustno pismo*) to the patient's chosen personal doctor; it carries findings, recommendations and, where relevant, limits on exertion. The hospital doctor prescribes the medicines needed for continued therapy. Before treatment ends the doctor informs the patient and, if needed, the family. — [ZZZS poster, Jan 2025](https://api.zzzs.si/ZZZS/info/egradiva.nsf/0/3cdf2381d29fef48c12585230041239f/$FILE/plakat%20Specialisti%C4%8Dno%20bolni%C5%A1ni%C4%8Dna%20dejavnost%20januar%202025.pdf)
- The personal doctor continues care in primary care or at home and issues the *delovni nalog* for physiotherapy; the same ZZZS leaflet states that eligible groups have a right to two preventive home visits by community nursing a year, with further nursing visits arranged with a doctor when needed. — [ZZZS poster, Jan 2025](https://api.zzzs.si/ZZZS/info/egradiva.nsf/0/3cdf2381d29fef48c12585230041239f/$FILE/plakat%20Specialisti%C4%8Dno%20bolni%C5%A1ni%C4%8Dna%20dejavnost%20januar%202025.pdf)
- Capacity limit on this step: 134,737 insured persons had no chosen personal doctor on 31 March 2026. — [Svet24 citing ZZZS, 15 Apr 2026](https://svet24.si/zdravje/134000-ljudi-brez-osebnega-zdravnika-1892975)
- Discharge letters and other documents reach the national record: all providers must submit documentation to CRPP (renamed CeZZ by ZDigZ). — [GOV.SI, 21 Nov 2025](https://www.gov.si/novice/2025-11-21-sprejeta-zakona-o-digitalizaciji-zdravstva-in-o-psihoterapevtski-dejavnosti/)

Patronažna služba (community nursing)
- NIJZ describes *patronažno zdravstveno varstvo* as the part of primary care that looks after individuals and families at home within a defined field area; its nurses are described as coordinators of all forms of help at home and as the link between patient and personal doctor. Curative visits are done on a doctor's order by *delovni nalog* and must be carried out no later than the next day; because curative orders cannot be capped, planned preventive visits often cannot be completed. — [NIJZ, "Patronažno zdravstveno varstvo", March 2024](https://nijz.si/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Patronazno-zdravstveno-varstvo_2024.pdf)
- Capacity (2022 data): 894 staff in community nursing, 820 of them field-area holders; 1,180,489 patient treatments in the year; a field area has on average 2,567 inhabitants (range 2,100–3,000). The preventive visit for older persons has had no ZZZS cap since 2021; programmes also cover chronic patients and fall-risk assessment for people over 65. — [NIJZ, March 2024](https://nijz.si/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Patronazno-zdravstveno-varstvo_2024.pdf)
- The same NIJZ document plans a web portal, to be set up as soon as possible, for notifying community nursing about discharges of mothers and newborns and about other patients who will need community nursing after discharge; it does not describe a uniform notification rule existing today. — [NIJZ, March 2024](https://nijz.si/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Patronazno-zdravstveno-varstvo_2024.pdf)
- Qualitative study of seven community nurses in one Štajerska health centre (interviews Dec 2018–Jan 2019): discharge information differs by hospital and ward and is not standardised; some wards send a nursing discharge sheet, some only a temporary slip, some nothing; phone notification is inconsistent; patients are sometimes told to call the service themselves and forget, especially at weekends; work orders are often incomplete; some hospitals have no discharge coordinator. The authors propose a single national information system and note that at the time CRPP was accessible only to doctors. — [Žele et al., Obzornik zdravstvene nege 2021; 55(1): 24–34](https://obzornik.zbornica-zveza.si/index.php/ObzorZdravNeg/article/download/3011/2957)
- The Ombudsman (*Varuh človekovih pravic*) found non-uniform notification of community nursing by maternity hospitals (12 of 14 replied; most notify only the local health centre); the Ministry and NIJZ said a uniform, digital notification method would be completed by mid-2025. This concerns maternity discharges, but shows the notification channel was not uniform. — [24ur (article date not shown; inquiry began 2023)](https://www.24ur.com/novice/slovenija/varuh-zaradi-neenakega-obvescanja-patronaznih-sluzb-o-odpustu-porodnic-krsena-ustava.html)
- zVEM authorisations can be entered by providers during home visits and community-nursing visits for patients with limited mobility, so the community nurse is already a recognised touchpoint for digital onboarding. — [eZdravje leaflet "zVEM pooblastila", Dec 2025](https://ezdrav.si/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/zVEM_pooblastila_A5_Dec2025_TRE_V6-1.pdf)

Relatives and informal carers
- About 75% of stroke patients return home, where relatives take on the burden (Tatjana Erjavec, 13 May 2025). — [RTV SLO/STA](https://www.rtvslo.si/dostopno/clanki/napovedi-glede-prihodnje-pojavnosti-mozganske-kapi-skrb-vzbujajoce/745070)
- URI Soča authors: relatives usually take over all care after discharge; discharge planning includes training the main helper; community health and social services are scarce. — [Rehabilitacija 2017, suppl. 1](https://ibmi.mf.uni-lj.si/rehabilitacija/vsebina/Rehabilitacija_2017_S1_p061-068.pdf)
- Formal recognition is narrow: the right *oskrbovalec družinskega člana* under the long-term-care act has applied since 1 January 2024; 1,758 people were providing it in August 2025 (3,462 applications by end of August 2025); payment is 1.2 times the minimum wage for one family member. Home long-term care and *e-oskrba* services started on 1 July 2025; institutional and day long-term care and the cash benefit on 1 December 2025. — [GOV.SI, 25 Sep 2025](https://www.gov.si/novice/2025-09-25-dolgotrajna-oskrba-skoraj-2000-oskrbovalcev-druzinskega-clana-1000-oseb-ze-z-oceno-upravicenosti/)
- IRSSV estimates (interim report, 1 Oct 2024): about 14,842 recipients of community nursing counted as long-term care in 2023; 8,668 users of home help (*pomoč družini na domu*) on 31 Dec 2022; 18,510 people in homes for older people on 31 Dec 2023; SHARE data show 4.0% of people aged 50+ needing at least one daily-living service and receiving no formal help. — [IRSSV, Strokovne podlage, 1 Oct 2024](https://www.gov.si/assets/ministrstva/MSP/Strokovne-podlage-Pregled-analize-stanja-na-podrocju-DO_IRSSV_vmesno-porocilo.pdf)
- Carer rules under long-term care as reported in February 2026: a family carer must leave the labour market and live at the same address; carers are entitled to 21 days of replacement care. — [N1, 22 Feb 2026](https://n1info.si/poglobljeno/tiha-epidemija-in-luknje-v-oskrbi-ki-jih-krpajo-svojci-ne-morem-ga-pustiti-samega/)

### Inferences
- Stakeholder map for a stroke scenario, with who can act: hospital/rehabilitation team (sets the monitoring plan at discharge, sees trends, is the natural alert recipient during the first weeks); personal doctor (receives *odpustno pismo*, is the only one who can issue *delovni nalog* for physiotherapy or community nursing, so alerts that need action in the community end there); community nurse (visits at home on the doctor's order, could see the trend before a visit and help with onboarding and proxy set-up); relative (helps the patient do the tasks, reports subjective observations, can act as proxy via zVEM *pooblastilo*).
- The strongest sourced "why now" lines for stroke: rehabilitation in Slovenia lasts at best about three months while guidelines want continuity for a year; the first systematic post-discharge check found in a Slovenian series came about four months after discharge and a third of patients did not attend; community nurses often do not get standardised discharge information.
- Staff-time argument (the brief's hint 2): a community nurse covers roughly 2,600 inhabitants and cannot cap curative orders, so any module must reduce, not add, routine contacts, e.g. by showing which patients are stable.
- For the roughly 135,000 people without a personal doctor the escalation path must be stated explicitly (e.g. hospital team or the health centre's clinic for unassigned patients), otherwise the alert has no owner.

### Gaps
- No general statistic on informal carers in Slovenia (share of adults providing care, hours) was found; EHIS/SURS/Eurocarers figures were searched for but not retrieved.
- The legal rule that defines who must be notified at discharge (the study cites a 2004 regulation) was not identified by name or read. Whether the planned discharge-notification portal for community nursing is live in 2026 was not verified.
- The exact rule on preventive community-nursing visits after stroke (number per year for chronic patients) was not found in a primary legal text; the "two preventive visits a year" statement comes from a ZZZS patient leaflet.
- No Slovenian data on readmissions or falls after stroke discharge were found.
- Whether community nurses now have CRPP/zVEM+ access (the 2021 study says they did not) was not verified.

## 2. Existing Slovenian telemedicine and e-health; where the platform would plug in

### Takeaway
Slovenia has a mature national backbone (eZdravje: CRPP, zVEM/zVEM+, eNaročanje, eRecept, TeleKap) and a new legal frame, the Zakon o digitalizaciji zdravstva (ZDigZ, in force 19 December 2025), which renames CRPP to CeZZ and moves central infrastructure to a state-owned company. Remote monitoring of patients is still project-based: the long-running CEZAR telemonitoring model was not yet accepted for regular financing in March 2025, and the Ministry's own plan lists digitised questionnaires/PROMs stored in CRPP and an interface for remote-monitoring devices as outputs still to be built.

### Cited Findings

eZdravje backbone (NIJZ deck, figures labelled 2025)
- NIJZ has managed eZdravje since 1 December 2015. — [NIJZ deck (uploaded May 2026)](https://nijz.si/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Digitalizacija-zdravstva-in-storitve-eZdravja-v-Sloveniji-Hajdi-Kosednar.pdf)
- CRPP (*Centralni register podatkov o pacientih*) holds the patient summary (*Povzetek podatkov o pacientu*, PPoP) and health documentation: about 200,000 new records a day and more than 330 million documents in total. — [NIJZ deck](https://nijz.si/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Digitalizacija-zdravstva-in-storitve-eZdravja-v-Sloveniji-Hajdi-Kosednar.pdf)
- zVEM (patient portal and app): about 560,000 active users, 360,000 of them on the mobile app. zVEMPlus (portal for health workers): more than 100,000 registered users by end 2023. — [NIJZ deck](https://nijz.si/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Digitalizacija-zdravstva-in-storitve-eZdravja-v-Sloveniji-Hajdi-Kosednar.pdf)

### Inferences
- Concrete plug-in story for a stroke post-discharge module (design direction built on documented features and plans; none of this is an existing integration):
  - Identity and onboarding: patient signs in with SI-PASS exactly as for zVEM (no new account); the hospital or community nurse helps at discharge, the way *digitočke* and in-visit authorisation entry already work.
  - Relatives: reuse *zVEM pooblastilo* rather than inventing a carer account; the proxy can already message the team and see documents, and proxy actions are logged. A carer's own observations would be a new data type on top of that.
  - Patient side in zVEM: the daily subjective questionnaire fits the planned questionnaire/PROM inbox; game-based measurements fit the planned remote-monitoring module's "manual entry and device capture" (the browser/phone is the device); reminders use the existing push/SMS/e-mail notification service.
  - Staff side in zVEM+: a patient list with trend and alert for the hospital or rehabilitation team, the personal doctor and the community nurse; follow-up via the existing secure messaging and the planned video consultation.
  - Start trigger: the *odpustno pismo* arriving in CRPP/CeZZ marks discharge; the monitoring plan could be attached to it.
  - Record: a periodic summary (trend, alerts, actions) sent to CRPP/CeZZ as a document so the personal doctor sees it next to the discharge letter.
  - Action: when an alert needs a visit, the personal doctor issues an *eNapotnica* or work order and the patient books through *eNaročanje*; *ePosvet* covers doctor-to-specialist questions.
- The national roadmap already contains a remote-monitoring pilot (diabetes) and a PROM inbox, so the pitch can be framed as "a second, stroke-specific use of modules NIJZ is already building" rather than a parallel system.
- The sponsor builds zVEM/zVEM+ and is inside the eKarton delivery group, so judges will know the framework well; claims about "integration with zVEM" should be phrased as a design direction, and user numbers should follow the sponsor's own slide (700,000+ and 3,600+) when presenting to them.
- Reimbursement is the weak point: as of the March 2025 Zdravstveni svet minutes, even the best-established telemonitoring model lacked regular financing. A hackathon team should say "no ZZZS tariff identified for this kind of remote monitoring" rather than assume one.

## 3. Interoperability expectations (Slovenian standards, EHDS, minimal FHIR mapping)

### Takeaway
The Ministry of Health states that the CRPP data model uses both openEHR and FHIR with shared code lists, alongside an XDS document repository, SNOMED CT/MKB-10 and LOINC for laboratories. EHDS (Regulation (EU) 2025/327) is in force since 26 March 2025, applies generally from 26 March 2027, and bites on EHR systems for the first priority data categories from 26 March 2029; for wellness apps it only requires a label when the maker claims interoperability with EHR systems.

### Cited Findings

Slovenian standards
- CRPP data model uses openEHR and FHIR with shared code lists (*šifranti*); supporting stores include an XDS document repository; laboratories must send a LOINC code with their local code; laboratory messaging uses openEHR and HL7v3. — [MZ eKarton presentation, 3 Mar 2026](https://www.gov.si/assets/ministrstva/MZ/DOKUMENTI/2-NOVICE/03032026_Konferenca-eZdravje-NOO-2026/eKarton.pdf)
- NIJZ lists SNOMED CT and MKB-10 (ICD-10) in the patient summary, standardised APIs and an XDS document database, and names adoption and enforcement of standards as a challenge. — [NIJZ deck (uploaded May 2026)](https://nijz.si/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Digitalizacija-zdravstva-in-storitve-eZdravja-v-Sloveniji-Hajdi-Kosednar.pdf)
- Title only (not fetched): an NIJZ-hosted Better document "CRPP 2.0" exists. — [NIJZ/Better PDF (title only)](https://nijz.si/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Better_CRPP-2.0.pdf)

EHDS timeline
- Published 5 March 2025, in force 26 March 2025; Commission implementing acts due by March 2027; first priority categories (patient summaries, ePrescriptions/eDispensations) exchangeable in all Member States from March 2029; second group (imaging, laboratory results, discharge reports) from March 2031; third countries may join HealthData@EU from March 2035. — [European Commission EHDS page](https://health.ec.europa.eu/ehealth-digital-health-and-care/european-health-data-space-regulation-ehds_en)
- Article 105: general application from 26 March 2027; Articles 3–15, 23(2)–(6), 25–27 and 47–49 apply from 26 March 2029 for the categories in Art. 14(1)(a)–(c) and matching EHR systems, and from 26 March 2031 for Art. 14(1)(d)–(f); most of Chapter IV (secondary use) from 26 March 2029. — [Regulation (EU) 2025/327, OJ text (mirror copy)](https://vsf-iwsold-pre-portal.sanidad.gob.es/areas/saludDigital/espacioEuropeoDS/docs/OJ_L_202500327_EN_TXT.pdf); official: [EUR-Lex ELI](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2025/327/oj/eng)
- Article 14(1) priority categories: patient summaries, electronic prescriptions, electronic dispensations, medical imaging studies and reports, medical test results, discharge reports. Article 15: the Commission must set the technical specifications of the European electronic health record exchange format by 26 March 2027. — [Regulation (EU) 2025/327](https://vsf-iwsold-pre-portal.sanidad.gob.es/areas/saludDigital/espacioEuropeoDS/docs/OJ_L_202500327_EN_TXT.pdf)

What EHDS asks of EHR systems
- An EHR system is software (or hardware plus software) that lets priority-category personal electronic health data be stored, intermediated, exported, imported, converted, edited or viewed and is intended by its manufacturer for healthcare providers in patient care or for patients accessing their data. Two harmonised components are mandatory: a European interoperability component and a European logging component. Manufacturers self-assess against Annex II, draw up technical documentation, test in European digital testing environments, issue an EU declaration of conformity, affix CE marking and register in the EU database before placing on the market. — [Osborne Clarke summary](https://www.osborneclarke.com/insights/eu-establishes-mandatory-ce-marking-electronic-health-record-systems)
- Recital-level statement: general-purpose software is not an EHR system even when used in healthcare. — [Regulation (EU) 2025/327, recitals 36/38](https://vsf-iwsold-pre-portal.sanidad.gob.es/areas/saludDigital/espacioEuropeoDS/docs/OJ_L_202500327_EN_TXT.pdf)
- Medical devices, IVDs and high-risk AI systems fall under the EHR interoperability requirements only if their manufacturer claims interoperability with EHR systems (Art. 27, recital 42). — [Regulation (EU) 2025/327](https://vsf-iwsold-pre-portal.sanidad.gob.es/areas/saludDigital/espacioEuropeoDS/docs/OJ_L_202500327_EN_TXT.pdf); [Osborne Clarke](https://www.osborneclarke.com/insights/eu-establishes-mandatory-ce-marking-electronic-health-record-systems)

What EHDS asks of wellness applications
- Definition (Art. 2(2)(ab)): software, or hardware with software, intended by the manufacturer for use by a natural person to process electronic health data, mainly to provide health information or to deliver care for purposes other than healthcare. — [Regulation (EU) 2025/327](https://vsf-iwsold-pre-portal.sanidad.gob.es/areas/saludDigital/espacioEuropeoDS/docs/OJ_L_202500327_EN_TXT.pdf)
- Art. 47: a wellness-app maker that claims interoperability with an EHR system must issue a label (validity at most three years). Art. 48: interoperability must not mean automatic sharing of health data with the EHR system; sharing needs the person's consent. Art. 49: labelled wellness apps and EHR systems are registered in a public EU database before market placement. Recital 49 calls this a labelling scheme, not certification. — [Regulation (EU) 2025/327](https://vsf-iwsold-pre-portal.sanidad.gob.es/areas/saludDigital/espacioEuropeoDS/docs/OJ_L_202500327_EN_TXT.pdf)
- Art. 86: Member States must ensure a particularly high level of protection for primary-use data; national law may require storage by healthcare providers within the Union. — [Regulation (EU) 2025/327](https://vsf-iwsold-pre-portal.sanidad.gob.es/areas/saludDigital/espacioEuropeoDS/docs/OJ_L_202500327_EN_TXT.pdf)

### Inferences
- A minimal, credible FHIR R4 mapping for this product (author's proposal, not from a Slovenian specification; resource names are from the public HL7 FHIR specification, pages not fetched this session, e.g. [Observation](https://hl7.org/fhir/R4/observation.html), [QuestionnaireResponse](https://hl7.org/fhir/R4/questionnaireresponse.html)):
  - Patient: pseudonymous platform ID; national identifiers (ZZZS number/EMŠO) only in a real deployment.
  - Questionnaire + QuestionnaireResponse: the daily questionnaire; one response per day.
  - Observation: one per game session with `code` = local code for the 0–100 motor score, `valueInteger`, `method`/`device` naming the game, `component[]` for sub-features (tremor index, path deviation, range of motion, reaction time), `interpretation` for the impairment class, `derivedFrom` pointing to the session's raw-feature Observation or QuestionnaireResponse.
  - Device: the software itself (name + version) and optionally the model version used for scoring, so each score is traceable to an algorithm version.
  - CarePlan: the monitoring plan (which games, frequency, baseline window, alert rule), linked to the discharge Encounter/Condition.
  - DetectedIssue or Flag: the negative-trend alert; DetectedIssue for the event with `evidence` linking the Observations, Flag for the persistent "needs review" marker on the patient list.
  - Communication (or Task): notification sent to the clinician and its acknowledgement.
  - Provenance/AuditEvent: who viewed what, matching the logging expectations in ZVOP-2/EHDS.
  - Terminology: no standard LOINC/SNOMED code is asserted here for a game-derived motor score; use a local CodeSystem and say so.
- Because the national model is openEHR-plus-FHIR with documents in XDS, the cheapest realistic integration story is "FHIR API on our side; a periodic summary document (PDF/structured) to CRPP/CeZZ; PROM-style questionnaire data when the national PROM channel exists".
- EHDS framing for a judge: the demo is not an EHR system in the Art. 2 sense unless it handles priority-category data for care; if it stays outside MDR as a wellness app, EHDS would only require a label if interoperability with EHR systems is claimed, and those articles apply from 26 March 2029 at the earliest.

### Gaps
- No public CRPP/CeZZ FHIR implementation guide or Slovenian national FHIR profiles were found; which FHIR version and which resources the national platform exposes is unverified.
- Status of the Commission's EHDS implementing acts (due by 26 March 2027) and of the Slovenian digital health authority/health data access body was not checked.
- IHE XDS use is evidenced only as "XDS repository/database" in two presentations; profile-level detail (XDS.b, MHD) was not found.

## 4. Medical-device status (MDR 2017/745, MDCG 2019-11 rev.1) and the EU AI Act

### Takeaway
Under MDCG 2019-11 rev.1 (17 June 2025), software that processes an individual patient's data beyond storage/communication for a medical purpose is medical device software, and Rule 11 puts software that informs diagnostic/therapeutic decisions or monitors physiological processes in class IIa or higher; class I is a narrow residual. A "wellness, not diagnosis" label works only if the intended purpose, claims and marketing are genuinely non-medical; the product as described (motor score, impairment class, alerts to clinicians for post-stroke/CIPN patients) reads as a medical purpose. Under the AI Act as amended in 2026, AI in medical devices that need a notified body is high-risk via Annex I, with obligations now applying from 2 August 2028.

### Cited Findings

MDCG 2019-11 rev.1
- Revision 1 was published by the Commission on 17 June 2025. — [European Commission, DG SANTE](https://health.ec.europa.eu/latest-updates/update-mdcg-2019-11-rev1-qualification-and-classification-software-regulation-eu-2017745-and-2025-06-17_en); [PDF](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/b45335c5-1679-4c71-a91c-fc7a4d37f12b_en?filename=mdcg_2019_11_en.pdf)
- Qualification steps (section 3.3): is it software; is it an Annex XVI device, accessory or driving software; does it perform an action on data beyond storage, archival, communication, simple search or lossless compression; is that action for the benefit of individual patients; does it meet the MDSW definition (software intended for a medical purpose under MDR/IVDR). — [MDCG 2019-11 rev.1](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/b45335c5-1679-4c71-a91c-fc7a4d37f12b_en?filename=mdcg_2019_11_en.pdf)
- Rule 11 as explained in section 4.2.1: (a) software providing information used for diagnostic or therapeutic decisions is class IIa, rising to IIb or III with the severity of possible harm; (b) software intended to monitor physiological processes is class IIa, or IIb for vital parameters where variations could cause immediate danger; (c) all other software is class I. Sub-rule (b) covers any physiological process, not only vital ones. — [MDCG 2019-11 rev.1](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/b45335c5-1679-4c71-a91c-fc7a4d37f12b_en?filename=mdcg_2019_11_en.pdf)
- Annex III table (IMDRF mapping for Rule 11(a)): even "non-serious condition / informs clinical management" maps to class IIa; "serious / drives management" is IIa; "serious / treat or diagnose" is IIb. — [MDCG 2019-11 rev.1](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/b45335c5-1679-4c71-a91c-fc7a4d37f12b_en?filename=mdcg_2019_11_en.pdf)
- Examples close to this product: software recommending personalised rehabilitation exercises for a musculoskeletal condition is MDSW; apps using daily questionnaires to personalise interventions are MDSW; a depression score computed from symptom inputs is class IIb under Rule 11(a); a risk score that triggers care processes is MDSW; telemedicine software that only transfers and displays monitoring data is not a device, but threshold alerts may be if they serve a medical purpose; routine or home monitoring of non-vital signals is class IIa under Rule 11(b). — [MDCG 2019-11 rev.1](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/b45335c5-1679-4c71-a91c-fc7a4d37f12b_en?filename=mdcg_2019_11_en.pdf)
- Class I examples given: a fertility-tracking app showing fertile/infertile status and a symbol-to-speech communication aid. — [MDCG 2019-11 rev.1](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/b45335c5-1679-4c71-a91c-fc7a4d37f12b_en?filename=mdcg_2019_11_en.pdf)
- Wellness or fitness apps with a non-medical purpose are not MDSW; intended purpose is what the manufacturer's supplied data say the device is for, and the risk of harm from malfunction is not by itself a qualification criterion. — [MDCG 2019-11 rev.1](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/b45335c5-1679-4c71-a91c-fc7a4d37f12b_en?filename=mdcg_2019_11_en.pdf)
- Commentary: intended purpose is read from labelling, instructions and marketing, so identical software can fall either side depending on claims; class I is described as a small residual category. — [OpenRegulatory explainer](https://openregulatory.com/mdcg/mdcg-2019-11)
- What rev.1 changed, per Emergo by UL (20 June 2025): no substantive change; introduces the term "medical device artificial intelligence (MDAI)"; asks for each module's intended purpose to be documented; adds examples, including software informing prognosis or prediction, and wellness/lifestyle illustrations; references EHDS. — [Emergo by UL](https://www.emergobyul.com/news/european-revision-primary-software-guidance-mdcg-2019-11-revision-1-small-changes-meaningful)
- EHR software that merely replaces paper records is not a device, while modules with a medical purpose (e.g. alerts) may qualify; modular products must state which modules are under MDR and which under other laws such as EHDS. — [MDCG 2019-11 rev.1](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/b45335c5-1679-4c71-a91c-fc7a4d37f12b_en?filename=mdcg_2019_11_en.pdf)

EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) after the 2026 "Digital Omnibus on AI"
- Timeline already passed: in force 1 August 2024; prohibitions and AI-literacy duty from 2 February 2025; general-purpose AI obligations from 2 August 2025; Article 50 transparency duties from 2 August 2026. — [Gibson Dunn, May 2026](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/)
- Provisional agreement on the omnibus was reached on 6 May 2026 and confirmed by the Council on 13 May 2026. — [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/)
- By 29 July 2026 the amendments were described as in force: Annex III high-risk obligations apply from 2 December 2027 (previously 2 August 2026); Annex I product-embedded high-risk obligations from 2 August 2028 (previously 2 August 2027). The amending act appears as Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 in the article's Official Journal link (number not stated in the article body; treat as to-be-verified). — [Orrick, 29 Jul 2026](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/07/EU-AI-Act-Update-Digital-Omnibus-Finalizes-8-Compliance-Changes)
- Medical devices: the final compromise kept MDR and IVDR in Annex I Section A, so AI-enabled devices remain high-risk under Article 6(1) where a notified body is involved in conformity assessment; devices eligible for self-certification (certain class I) may fall outside that trigger; the "safety component" notion was narrowed so AI used only for assistance or workflow convenience is not high-risk unless failure endangers health or safety. — [Taylor Wessing, 2026](https://www.taylorwessing.com/en/international-life-sciences-newsletter/life-sciences-legal-lens-vol-2/update-ai-enabled-medical-devices-and-ivds-confirmed-as-high-risk)
- Annex III lists eight areas; the only healthcare-related entries are eligibility for public services including healthcare (5(a)) and emergency healthcare patient triage (5(d)); general clinical monitoring or decision support is not listed there. — [artificialintelligenceact.eu, Annex III](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/annex/3/)
- The Commission's December 2025 MDR/IVDR simplification proposal (COM(2025) 1023) would make the device rules the primary framework for AI in devices. — [Taylor Wessing, 2026](https://www.taylorwessing.com/en/international-life-sciences-newsletter/life-sciences-legal-lens-vol-2/update-ai-enabled-medical-devices-and-ivds-confirmed-as-high-risk)

### Inferences
- Applying the MDCG steps to the product as described: it is software; it computes scores and trends (more than storage/communication); it does so for individual patients; its described purpose (detecting motor deterioration after stroke, surgery or chemotherapy and alerting staff) matches the MDR notions of monitoring/prediction of disease. That is the profile of MDSW, most plausibly Rule 11 class IIa (information used for clinical management decisions; arguably 11(b) if motor function is treated as a physiological process), with IIb arguable if a missed deterioration could lead to serious harm. This is a reading of the guidance, not a determination.
- What the "monitoring change against own baseline, not diagnosis" framing can do: keep the claim modest, avoid diagnostic labels, and position the hackathon build as a research/demonstration prototype not placed on the market. What it cannot do: remove a medical purpose. The guidance qualifies by intended purpose, and monitoring plus clinician alerts in named patient groups is a medical purpose whatever the word "diagnosis" is replaced with; the output "impairment class" in particular reads as diagnostic information.
- A genuinely non-medical variant would have to drop disease-specific claims and clinical alerting (e.g. a general dexterity self-tracking tool), which is a different product from the one pitched.
- Honest pitch wording: "prototype, not CE-marked; if developed for clinical use we expect it to be medical device software, likely class IIa under Rule 11, requiring a notified body; that in turn would make the ML component high-risk AI under AI Act Annex I, with obligations applying from 2 August 2028."
- AI Act duties with dates already in force that are relevant even to a demo: transparency toward people interacting with an AI system (Article 50, from 2 August 2026) and the softened AI-literacy expectation.

### Gaps
- The Official Journal entry for the omnibus (number, publication and entry-into-force dates) was not read directly; "Regulation (EU) 2026/1744" comes from a link in a law-firm article and from search-result titles ([Cuatrecasas](https://www.cuatrecasas.com/en/spain/intellectual-property/art/digital-omnibus-ai-has-been-published), [Garrigues](https://www.garrigues.com/en_GB/garrigues-digital/ai-digital-omnibus-regulation-has-been-published-redefining-deadlines-and), titles only).

- The MDR text itself (Art. 2(1) definition, Annex VIII Rule 11, Art. 5(5) in-house devices of health institutions, clinical-investigation rules) was not fetched; statements above rely on MDCG guidance and commentary.
- The MDCG/AIB joint guidance on MDR–AI Act interplay (referenced in a footnote of MDCG 2019-11 rev.1) was not read.
- Slovenian competent-authority (JAZMP) guidance on software qualification was not searched.

## 5. GDPR Art. 9, ZVOP-2, ZPacP, ZZPPZ: privacy and what a demo should state

### Takeaway
Health data are a special category under GDPR Article 9; the usable exceptions for this product are explicit consent (9(2)(a)) or provision of health care under law or a contract with a health professional bound by secrecy (9(2)(h) with 9(3)), and Member States may add conditions (9(4)). A DPIA is the expected step: the Slovenian Information Commissioner's criteria include health data, patients as vulnerable persons, new technologies and scoring, and the Commissioner has fined for missing DPIAs. On-device video processing is the strongest privacy-by-design argument the team has, but landmarks and scores remain health data.

### Cited Findings
- Article 9(1) GDPR prohibits processing of health data and of biometric data used to uniquely identify a person unless an exception applies; 9(2)(a) explicit consent for specified purposes; 9(2)(h) medical diagnosis and provision of health care under law or a contract with a health professional, subject to 9(3) professional secrecy; 9(2)(j) scientific research under Article 89(1) with a legal basis and safeguards; 9(4) lets Member States keep or add conditions for genetic, biometric and health data. — [GDPR Art. 9 (gdpr-info.eu reproduction)](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-9-gdpr/)
- Article 35 GDPR: a DPIA is needed before processing likely to result in high risk, in particular with new technologies; it is specifically required for large-scale processing of special-category data and for systematic, extensive automated evaluation of personal aspects with significant effects; it must describe the processing and purposes, assess necessity and proportionality, assess risks and list mitigating measures; each supervisory authority publishes a list of operations requiring a DPIA. — [GDPR Art. 35 (gdpr-info.eu reproduction)](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-35-gdpr/)
- Slovenian Information Commissioner (Informacijski pooblaščenec; presentation by Andrej Tomšič, 7 May 2026): the Commissioner has published a list of processing operations requiring a DPIA; criteria include health data, innovative technologies, vulnerable individuals such as patients and older people, large scale, profiling/scoring, automated decisions and combining datasets; a DPIA does not replace a missing legal basis and should not be a marketing document; of 58 GDPR fines cited, 7 were standalone fines for not carrying out a DPIA. — [IP presentation hosted by University of Ljubljana, 7 May 2026](https://www.uni-lj.si/assets/Sluzba-za-knjiznicno-dejavnost/DPIA-in-umetna-inteligenca-v-raziskavah-ki-vkljucujejo-osebne-podatke-v2.pdf)
- Same presentation, AI-specific risks the Commissioner highlights: human review that is not meaningful, unfair outcomes from unrepresentative training data, model drift; recommendations include bias audits before deployment and continuous monitoring afterwards. — [IP presentation, 7 May 2026](https://www.uni-lj.si/assets/Sluzba-za-knjiznicno-dejavnost/DPIA-in-umetna-inteligenca-v-raziskavah-ki-vkljucujejo-osebne-podatke-v2.pdf)
- ZVOP-2 structure (table of contents of the published commentary): Art. 8 child's consent for information-society services; Art. 22 keeping a processing log (*vodenje dnevnika obdelave*); Art. 23 security of personal data in special processing; Art. 24 impact assessment and prior consultation; Arts. 65–67 transfers; Arts. 68–72 research processing; Arts. 81–84 biometrics. — [GV Založba, ZVOP-2 commentary contents](https://gvzalozba.si/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/knjiga-Zakon-ZVOP-2_SPLET.pdf)
- NIJZ names ZZPPZ (Art. 14.b), ZPacP, ZVOP-2 and GDPR as the legal bases of eZdravje and CRPP. — [NIJZ deck](https://nijz.si/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Digitalizacija-zdravstva-in-storitve-eZdravja-v-Sloveniji-Hajdi-Kosednar.pdf)
- ZDigZ gives patients the ability to block a given provider or individual from accessing their documentation in CeZZ; the Information Commissioner criticised centralisation and widening of purposes during adoption. — [GOV.SI, 21 Nov 2025](https://www.gov.si/novice/2025-11-21-sprejeta-zakona-o-digitalizaciji-zdravstva-in-o-psihoterapevtski-dejavnosti/); [RTV SLO, Nov 2025](https://www.rtvslo.si/n/763221)
- EHDS Art. 86 allows national law to require that primary-use health data held by healthcare providers be stored within the Union; Art. 48 requires consent before a wellness app shares data with an EHR system. — [Regulation (EU) 2025/327](https://vsf-iwsold-pre-portal.sanidad.gob.es/areas/saludDigital/espacioEuropeoDS/docs/OJ_L_202500327_EN_TXT.pdf)

### Inferences
- Lawful-basis story by stage: (1) hackathon demo with synthetic or team members' own data: say no real patient data are processed; (2) pilot/research: explicit consent (Art. 6(1)(a) + 9(2)(a)) or the research route, with ethics approval; (3) clinical service run by a hospital or health centre: the provider is controller, the platform vendor a processor under an Art. 28 contract, and the basis is care provision under Art. 9(2)(h) and Slovenian health law, so consent is then not the main basis for the care record.
- On-device video argument: MediaPipe runs in the browser; raw webcam frames never leave the device and are not stored; only derived hand/arm landmarks or aggregated features (range of motion, tremor, speed) and the score are transmitted. Limits to state: landmark time-series are still personal health data once linked to a patient; they are not "biometric data for unique identification" in the Art. 9 sense unless used to identify someone, but re-identification risk from motion signatures should be acknowledged rather than dismissed.
- Data-minimisation statements a demo can credibly make: no video or images uploaded; features rather than raw sensor streams where possible; pseudonymous study ID separated from identity; EU-region hosting; encryption in transit and at rest; role-based access with an access log the patient can ask about; defined retention and deletion; per-patient baseline means no cross-patient profiling is needed for the alert; a clinician reviews every alert (no solely automated decision with significant effect, cf. GDPR Art. 22).
- DPIA: with health data, patients, new technology (camera/sensor-based scoring with ML) and scoring/profiling, several of the Commissioner's criteria are met at once, so the team should say a DPIA would be done before any pilot, not that one is unnecessary.
- Avoid claiming "anonymous": the dataset is at best pseudonymised while the clinician side can see who the patient is.

### Gaps
- ZVOP-2 article texts were not read (PISRS requires JavaScript; Uradni list was unreachable). Unverified recollections to check before use: ZVOP-2 published in Uradni list RS 163/22 and applicable from 26 January 2023; age of digital consent 15 (Art. 8); processing logs for special-category data to be kept for a fixed period (Art. 22); encryption expectations for transmission of special-category data (Art. 23).
- ZPacP (patient-rights act) provisions on access to and confidentiality of health documentation, and ZZPPZ provisions beyond Art. 14.b, were not read; article numbers are not confirmed.
- The Information Commissioner's actual DPIA list and any Commissioner guidance specific to telemedicine, health apps or camera-based processing were not retrieved; only the 2026 presentation was read.
- No Slovenian rule requiring health data to be hosted in Slovenia was found; hosting practice for public providers (state cloud, zNET) was not researched.
- EDPB guidance on health apps/wearables and on anonymisation of motion data was not consulted.
