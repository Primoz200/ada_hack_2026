## 1. Which commercial and academic platforms do post-discharge / remote monitoring with patient self-reporting and clinician dashboards?

### Takeaway
The market splits into four families that barely overlap: general RPM / virtual-ward platforms (vital signs + questionnaires + red-amber-green alerts), oncology ePRO systems (symptom questionnaires + threshold alerts + nurse triage), hardware-based neuro/MSK telerehabilitation (therapy games + therapist portal), and digital motor biomarkers for MS/Parkinson's (active smartphone tests + trend reports). In Europe, alert-generating monitoring software is normally a class IIa (or higher) medical device, which matters for how a hackathon prototype may describe itself.

### Cited Findings

**A. General RPM / virtual-ward / post-discharge platforms**

- Regulatory rule of thumb (MHRA advice quoted by NICE): these platforms are likely class IIa or higher; software that continuously/automatically monitors vital signs and warns of deterioration is likely class IIb; software takes the highest class of any connected device. — [NICE HTG697 ch. 2](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/HTG697/chapter/2-the-technology)

### Inferences
- The clinician-side pattern is the same everywhere it works: a worklist sorted by risk colour, threshold or trend alerts, and a named nurse/therapist who acts. Copying that pattern is lower risk than inventing a new one.
- Motor-biomarker products (Floodlight, Neu Health, StrivePD) prove that regulators and health systems accept smartphone motor tests feeding clinician trend reports, but all sit in chronic neurology (MS, Parkinson's), not in post-discharge follow-up.
- Rehab-game vendors depend on proprietary hardware (gloves, pucks, robots, depth cameras). A browser-only tool using a mouse, webcam and phone sensors has a distribution advantage but none of these vendors' clinical validation.
- Several well-funded RPM businesses struggled commercially (Best Buy's write-down and exit from Current Health; MindMaze's restructuring; Lenus and Current Health leaving the NHS market). A pitch should not assume that a good product alone produces a viable business.
- Since monitoring software that drives clinical alerts falls under medical-device rules (class IIa or higher per MHRA advice), the hackathon demo should be labelled a research prototype, not a diagnostic.

## 2. What features are common to effective RPM/ePRO programmes, and what do systematic reviews and RCTs (2019–2026) show?

### Takeaway
Benefit appears when a clinician is alerted and acts; apps without clinician review show no readmission effect. The most consistent gains are fewer emergency visits and better quality of life and symptom control; effects on readmission are mixed and the survival benefit seen in early oncology trials was not reproduced in the large PRO-TECT trial.

### Cited Findings

**Guideline consensus on features**
- ESMO Clinical Practice Guideline (Ann Oncol 2022, DOI 10.1016/j.annonc.2022.04.007): digital symptom monitoring during systemic cancer treatment is recommended at the highest evidence grade [I, A]. Required features, also [I, A]: automated alerts to clinicians for severe or worsening symptoms, electronic reminders to patients (email, text or automated call), and trained nurses or allied staff as first responders. Other points: keep item numbers limited (successful weekly systems used 10 to 20 items), offer more than one reporting mode, let carers report on the patient's behalf, and give nurses protected time, because without workflow integration and designated staff benefit is unlikely. — [ESMO guideline full text (Univ. Torino repository)](https://iris.unito.it/retrieve/e27ce435-691b-2581-e053-d805fe0acbaa/PIIS0923753422006913.pdf)

**Oncology ePRO**
- Meta-analysis of 15 RCTs (about 5,446 patients; JMIR, 31 Jul 2024): small pooled improvement in health-related quality of life at about 6 months (SMD 0.21, 95% CI 0.11 to 0.30; 8 studies) and at 3 months on FACT-G (SMD 0.29, 0.19 to 0.39; 5 studies). Survival and hospital use were too inconsistently reported to pool. Among systems with clinician alerts, 6 of 8 improved quality of life and 6 of 9 improved symptoms; tailored patient advice plus clinician alerts were associated with benefit (association only). Patient adherence across trials ranged from 50% to 100%. — [Perry et al., JMIR 2024;26:e49089](https://www.jmir.org/2024/1/e49089)
- PRO-TECT final results (Nature Medicine 2025; 52 US community practices, 1,191 patients with metastatic cancer, weekly ePRO with alerts): no survival difference (HR 0.99, 95% CI 0.83 to 1.17); fewer emergency visits (HR 0.84, P = 0.03); delayed deterioration of physical function (HR 0.73), symptoms (HR 0.69) and quality of life (HR 0.72); 91.5% of 22,486 expected weekly surveys completed. Taken from a secondary summary, not the journal page. — [OncoDaily summary, Feb 2025](https://oncodaily.com/insight/ethan-basch-241583)
- eRAPID (Leeds, 508 patients on chemotherapy, weekly reporting for 18 weeks, automated severity-based self-management advice, reports in the electronic record): better physical well-being at 6 weeks (P = .028) and 12 weeks (P = .039) but not 18 weeks; better self-efficacy at 18 weeks (P = .0073); average weekly compliance 64.7%; investigators concluded it did not add hospital workload. — [ASCO Post, Jan 2021](https://ascopost.com/news/january-2021/electronic-patient-self-reporting-of-adverse-events-during-cancer-chemotherapy-erapid-system/)
- CAPRI (single-centre phase 3 RCT, 559 evaluable patients on oral anticancer drugs, nurse navigators + app with automated alerts, 6 months): relative dose intensity 93.4% vs 89.4% (P = 0.04); grade ≥3 toxicity 27.6% vs 36.9% (P = 0.02); mean hospital days 2.82 vs 4.44 (P = 0.02); emergency visits 15.1% vs 22.0% (P = 0.04); no difference in global quality of life or survival. — [Mir et al., Nat Med 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-022-01788-1)
- MSK InSight Care, daily monitoring (217 patients): an assessment containing a red alert was followed by an emergency visit or admission within 7 days 8.7% of the time vs 2.9% without, and risk rose with the number of red alerts (22.9% with five or more). Observational; shows alerts carry signal, not that the programme prevents events. — [Daly et al., JAMA Netw Open 2022](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2789617)

**Post-surgical monitoring**
- Meta-analysis of perioperative telehealth after abdominal surgery (19 studies: 11 RCTs, 8 cohorts; 10,536 patients; JAMA Netw Open 2024, DOI 10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2024.8555): emergency visits RR 0.78 (95% CI 0.65 to 0.94); readmissions RR 0.67 (0.58 to 0.78); complications unchanged, RR 1.05 (0.77 to 1.43). — [Grygorian et al., abstract via QxMD](https://read.qxmd.com/read/38669018/digital-health-interventions-and-patient-safety-in-abdominal-surgery-a-systematic-review-and-meta-analysis); [AHRQ PSNet entry](https://psnet.ahrq.gov/issue/digital-health-interventions-and-patient-safety-abdominal-surgery-systematic-review-and-meta)
- Conference abstract, American Society of Anesthesiologists annual meeting, reported 19 Oct 2026 (38 studies, 7,507 patients; not yet peer-reviewed; no confidence intervals in the report): post-operative monitoring apps were associated with 22% lower risk of major complications, 22% lower risk of emergency visits and 15% lower risk of readmission; total complications did not fall. The readmission reduction appeared only where a nurse or surgical-team member reviewed the submissions and could act; programmes without clinician review showed none. Most programmes used daily check-ins on pain, fever, nausea and wound appearance. — [News-Medical, 19 Oct 2026](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20261019/Postoperative-monitoring-apps-reduce-emergency-visits-and-complications.aspx)
- MSK Recovery Tracker (retrospective before/after cohort, 7,165 ambulatory cancer surgery patients): odds of an urgent-care visit without readmission fell (OR 0.78, 95% CI 0.60 to 1.00; P = .047); readmissions unchanged; nursing calls rose by 0.86 per patient (a 34% increase). — [Simon et al., JAMA Surg 2021 (MSK repository record)](https://synapse.mskcc.org/synapse/works/186153)
- University of Edinburgh systematic review in npj Digital Medicine (44 studies of digital monitoring after surgical discharge, as reported by Healthcare IT News): tools detected complications early and reduced hospital resource use, but heterogeneous methods and poor reporting (data security, cost, patient involvement) limited conclusions; most interventions covered the first 30 days. — [Healthcare IT News](https://www.healthcareitnews.com/news/digital-health-tools-can-be-useful-post-surgery-more-high-quality-research-needed)

**General post-acute RPM and virtual wards**
- Systematic review with meta-analysis of remote vital-sign monitoring in hospital-at-home and post-acute medical patients (28 studies; JMIR, 26 May 2025): 60-day readmission not significantly reduced (RR 0.81, 95% CI 0.61 to 1.09; 6 studies); 30-day mortality lower (RR 0.65, 0.42 to 0.99; 4 studies); certainty very low for both. Authors stress the need for defined thresholds and response protocols and note that symptoms often precede vital-sign changes. — [Cheng et al., JMIR 2025;27:e64753](https://www.jmir.org/2025/1/e64753)
- Systematic review of telemonitoring and readmission in heart failure/COPD (29 studies, 4,326 patients; Sept 2024): 14 studies showed benefit, 14 neutral, 1 harm. COPD studies more often collected symptom questionnaires alongside vitals (55% vs 17% of heart-failure studies), and COPD showed more readmission benefit; authors suggest patient-reported symptoms help catch decompensation earlier and that recently discharged patients benefit most. No meta-analysis. — [Stergiopoulos et al., Front Digit Health 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/digital-health/articles/10.3389/fdgth.2024.1441334/full)
- Older meta-analysis (10 RCTs to Jan 2017, 4,820 adults within three months of discharge): virtual-ward care lowered mortality (RR 0.59) and heart-failure readmission (RR 0.61) in heart failure, with no benefit in other chronic diseases. Summary dated 2018. — [NIHR Evidence alert, 2018](https://evidence.nihr.ac.uk/alert/virtual-wards-reduce-readmissions-in-people-after-hospitalisation-for-heart-failure/)
- NHS England South East evaluation (29 virtual wards, snapshot 26 Feb 2024): about 9,165 avoided non-elective admissions a year; one avoided admission per 2.5 virtual-ward admissions on average (1:1 in mature wards); net benefit £10.4m a year (£24.5m gross benefit, £14.2m cost). Success factors: clinical leadership, daily multidisciplinary rounds, adequate staffing, digital integration, single point of referral. — [NHS England](https://www.england.nhs.uk/long-read/summary-of-south-east-region-virtual-wards-evaluation/)
- Null result worth citing honestly: the GetWell Loop stepped-wedge trial (5,490 discharges) found no readmission reduction, with app activation of 350/2,755. — [Am J Manag Care 2024](https://www.ajmc.com/scaling-care-coordination-through-digital-engagement-stepped-wedge-trial-assessing-readmissions)

**Stroke telerehabilitation (context for the stroke use case)**
- Review of synchronous home-based upper-limb telerehabilitation after stroke (Healthcare, Jan 2025): pooled Fugl-Meyer upper-extremity gain of 4.70 points (95% CI 2.67 to 6.73) in the pooled trials, above the 4.25-point minimal clinically important difference; heterogeneous designs; qualitative studies name gamification and score tracking as motivators. The extraction noted an inconsistent study count (30 vs 32). — [Stangenberg-Gliss et al., Healthcare 2025;13(1):90](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-9032/13/1/90)

**Clinician workload**
- Dutch hypertension telemonitoring programme (174 users, 30,184 measurements, Jun 2022 to May 2023): estimated workload 6.2 minutes per user per month when 79% of alerts were handled automatically. — [van Steenkiste et al., JMIR Med Inform 2025;13:e66066](https://medinform.jmir.org/2025/1/e66066)
- Commentary on the MSK daily programme estimated about 1,216 nursing hours (roughly 0.6 full-time equivalent) for the programme's alert volume at 10 minutes per alert. — [Rocque, JAMA Netw Open 2022](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2789621)

### Inferences
- The pitchable, defensible claim is: clinician-reviewed remote monitoring after surgery is associated with roughly a fifth fewer emergency visits (RR 0.78 in the 2024 meta-analysis) and, in abdominal surgery, a third fewer readmissions (RR 0.67). It should be paired with the caveat that app-only programmes show no effect.
- Do not claim a survival benefit: the largest trial (PRO-TECT) found none.
- Feature checklist backed by evidence or guideline grade: (1) threshold and worsening-trend alerts, (2) a named nurse/therapist first responder, (3) automated reminders, (4) automated self-management advice to the patient (eRAPID, Kaiku, Recovery Tracker feedback), (5) short questionnaires, (6) carer proxy reporting and a non-app fallback.
- Combining symptom questions with objective measures is supported indirectly: reviews in heart failure/COPD and post-acute care both note that symptoms often move before vital signs, and NeuroDetect's combined model had the highest AUC.

### Gaps
- No RCT or systematic review of post-discharge remote monitoring specific to stroke readmission, carpal tunnel release or CIPN was found; a 2024 stroke telerehabilitation meta-analysis (PubMed 39172060) and a 2024 review of upper-limb tele-assessment after stroke (PMC11487868) could not be opened.
- Exact numbers from the Edinburgh npj Digital Medicine review and from the PRO-TECT journal article were not read at source.
- NICE has not, in the pages I opened, published cost figures for virtual-ward platforms; I found no NICE guidance specific to digital stroke or hand rehabilitation in this session (not searched exhaustively).
- Quantified effect of onboarding quality on outcomes: not found.

## 3. Why do RPM programmes fail?

### Takeaway
The recurring causes are low activation and decaying adherence, alert volumes that swamp staff (most alerts are administrative or non-actionable), payment rules that do not match real usage, weak workflow integration, and digital exclusion of older patients. All five have published numbers.

### Cited Findings

**Activation, adherence decay and drop-out**
- Meta-analysis of app-based chronic-disease interventions (17 studies): pooled drop-out 43% (95% CI 29 to 57); 49% in observational studies vs 40% in RCTs. — [Meyerowitz-Katz et al., JMIR 2020;22(9):e20283](https://www.jmir.org/2020/9/e20283/)
- Medly heart-failure telemonitoring (231 patients, daily morning readings): full adherence 81.2% in month 1 and 63.1% in month 12, about 1.4 percentage points lost per month; significant decline from month 3. Older patients were more adherent (+3.49 points per decade of age, P = .04). Of 61 who left, 28% left by choice. — [Ware et al., JMIR mHealth uHealth 2019;7(2):e13259](https://mhealth.jmir.org/2019/2/e13259)
- MSK daily cancer symptom monitoring, no reminders: mean 3.9 submissions per week in the first 6 months, 3.5 in months 6 to 12, 2.7 after one year; weekday response 58.4% vs weekend 51.3%. — [Daly et al., JAMA Netw Open 2022](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2789617)
- Post-discharge check-in app offered at scale: only 12.7% of eligible patients activated it (350/2,755; 10.0% among high-risk patients). — [Am J Manag Care 2024](https://www.ajmc.com/scaling-care-coordination-through-digital-engagement-stepped-wedge-trial-assessing-readmissions)
- Lung-surgery app pilot (50 patients): 54% completed any post-operative health check, median 3 surveys per patient over 30 days. — [Kneuertz et al., J Thorac Dis 2020](https://jtd.amegroups.org/article/view/35930/html)
- Noona in routine US use: under 30% of active accounts had used the symptom questionnaire. — [JMIR Cancer 2022](https://cancer.jmir.org/2022/2/e29292)
- Germany's prescribable apps (DiGA), industry report covering Oct 2020 to Dec 2024: about 17% of activation codes were never redeemed (vs about 10% for medicines), and patients took about 14 days from prescription to first use; about 870,000 codes redeemed; 59 apps listed. — [Deutsches Ärzteblatt, 31 Mar 2025](https://www.aerzteblatt.de/news/diga-report-apps-auf-rezept-knapp-eine-million-mal-genutzt-fe05cccf-1cd0-47a3-9b9d-eff971ca3cfa)
- Home stroke gamified rehab: 6 of 23 starters dropped out or were lost over about 19 weeks. — [JMIR Serious Games 2025](https://games.jmir.org/2025/1/e69335)

**Alert burden**
- Dutch hypertension programme: 17,293 alerts from 30,184 measurements (57%); 79% auto-processed (mostly overdue/inactivity alerts); of 2,101 manual follow-up actions, 77% were administrative with no clinical impact and 23% clinical; 15% of users generated 61% of simple manual alerts. Raising thresholds in simulation cut alerts by about half. — [van Steenkiste et al., JMIR Med Inform 2025](https://medinform.jmir.org/2025/1/e66066)
- MSK daily programme: 50.3% of assessments triggered at least one alert (23,112 alerts from 14,603 assessments); 45.1% of red alerts had no yellow alert for that symptom in the preceding week, i.e. nearly half of severe events arrived without warning. — [Daly et al., JAMA Netw Open 2022](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2789617)
- Post-surgical symptom tracker increased nursing calls by 34% while leaving readmissions unchanged. — [Simon et al., JAMA Surg 2021 (MSK repository record)](https://synapse.mskcc.org/synapse/works/186153)
- NICE asks virtual-ward evaluations to report alert counts including false positives and false negatives, i.e. this is still an evidence gap for commercial platforms. — [NICE HTE13 ch. 1](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/hte13/chapter/1-Recommendations)

**Reimbursement and business model**
- US Medicare: device-supply billing (CPT 99454) required 16 or more days of readings per month; from 2026 a new code (99445) pays for 2 to 15 days and a new code covers 10 to 19 minutes of management time. — [Medical Economics, 18 Dec 2025](https://www.medicaleconomics.com/view/remote-patient-monitoring-in-2026-new-rules-from-medicare)
- France: telemonitoring entered standard reimbursement on 1 July 2023. Monthly per-patient fees: operator fee €11 (level 1) or €28 (level 2); technical fee for the digital device €50 (organisational benefit), €73.33 (quality-of-life benefit), €82.50 (morbidity benefit) or €91.67 (mortality benefit). Listed indications on the page: heart, kidney and respiratory failure, diabetes, gestational diabetes, cardiac implants. — [sante.fr](https://www.sante.fr/node/6925603)
- France, oncology: HAS gave a favourable opinion on reimbursing cancer follow-up apps (reported 7 Dec 2023), estimating up to about one million patients a year could benefit (article paywalled beyond the summary). — [Le Quotidien du Pharmacien, Dec 2023](https://www.lequotidiendupharmacien.fr/exercice-pro/politique-de-sante/des-applications-de-suivi-des-malades-du-cancer-prises-en-charge-par-lassurance-maladie)
- Belgium, oncology telemonitoring reimbursed from 1 Jan 2026: €84.89 for the start-up month, then €59.89 a month for up to four further months, limited to patients on certain targeted (ATC L01F) drugs; funding stops after five months. — [Le Spécialiste, 4 Feb 2026](https://www.lespecialiste.be/fr/actualites/e-health/telesurveillance-oncologique-le-remboursement-fait-son-entree-en-2026.html)
- Market signal: Best Buy's chief executive said the care-at-home market was not scaling as fast as forecast; uncertainty over the US hospital-at-home payment waiver slowed adoption. — [Fierce Healthcare, 2025](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/health-tech/whats-next-current-health-after-best-buy-divests-care-home-company)
- PHTI found that adding remote-therapeutic-monitoring billing to physical therapy raises net spending (by US$1.7m per million insured lives in its model) because avoided care does not offset the monitoring fees. — [PHTI, Jun 2024](https://phti.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2024/06/PHTI-Virtual-MSK-Solutions-Executive-Summary.pdf)

**Workflow integration**
- ESMO: without clinical integration, designated personnel and protected nurse time, benefits of symptom monitoring are unlikely. — [ESMO guideline](https://iris.unito.it/retrieve/e27ce435-691b-2581-e053-d805fe0acbaa/PIIS0923753422006913.pdf)
- Stroke rehab professionals in three EU countries expressed concern that a home system would increase their workload. — [JMIR Rehabil Assist Technol 2026](https://rehab.jmir.org/2026/1/e79382)
- Many acute events in the MSK programme happened even though the team already knew about the alert, i.e. detection without an effective response pathway does not prevent admissions. — [Rocque commentary, JAMA Netw Open 2022](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2789621)

**Digital exclusion**
- Background figures quoted in a 2025 systematic review on older adults and digital health (UK-centred, from external sources): adults 65+ are 18 times more likely than 18 to 24-year-olds never to have used a health app; 34% of people 75+ are offline; 13% of people 65+ own no mobile phone; about 4.7 million UK over-65s lack basic digital skills. — [Hepburn et al., JMIR Aging 2025;8:e80000](https://aging.jmir.org/2025/1/e80000)
- ESMO cites a study in which over 35% of patients preferred automated telephone reporting to the web, linked to older age and lower education; it recommends paper/staff-assisted fallback and carer proxy reporting. — [ESMO guideline](https://iris.unito.it/retrieve/e27ce435-691b-2581-e053-d805fe0acbaa/PIIS0923753422006913.pdf)
- Counterpoint: once enrolled, older patients can be the more adherent group (Medly). — [Ware et al., 2019](https://mhealth.jmir.org/2019/2/e13259)

### Inferences
- The biggest loss is at the front door (activation), not later: the 12.7% activation in the Wisconsin trial versus over 90% weekly completion among consented trial participants in PRO-TECT suggests that getting patients enrolled and started before discharge matters more than anything done later. (That PRO-TECT's high completion was due to its onboarding is my reading, not a finding of either study.)
- Auto-handling "no data today" alerts and reserving human attention for clinical alerts is the design lesson from the Dutch data; trend-against-own-baseline alerts, as the team plans, are one way to keep volume down, but nearly half of severe events in the MSK data had no warning the week before, so trend alerts will not catch everything.
- For a European pitch, reimbursement exists and is priced: France pays roughly €60 to €120 per patient-month in total (operator + technical fee, my addition of the listed components) and Belgium about €60 to €85 per patient-month for oncology. These are usable anchors for a business model slide.

### Gaps
- Participant-level refusal rates for RPM among elderly patients at discharge: not found (the JMIR Aging review reports study-level barriers only).
- Alert positive-predictive values for commercial virtual-ward platforms: not found; NICE lists it as evidence still to be generated.
- Slovenian reimbursement or deployment data: not researched.
- Germany's 2026 statutory-insurer DiGA report (page did not load) and the "1.6 million prescriptions" headline [search title only] were not verified.

## 4. Is there any product that combines gamified objective motor assessment with routine post-discharge symptom reporting in one clinician dashboard for stroke/CTR/CIPN? What is the genuine white space?

### Takeaway
I found no commercial or academic product that combines all four elements: short routine symptom check-ins, hardware-free gamified motor tests, a per-patient trend against the patient's own baseline, and clinician alerts, for stroke, carpal tunnel release or CIPN. The nearest neighbours each cover two or three elements in other conditions or with other hardware.

### Cited Findings
- Nearest for CIPN: NeuroDetect pairs a neuropathy questionnaire with phone-sensor motor tasks (including finger tapping), but it is iPhone-only research software, assessed every 2 to 4 weeks, with no clinician dashboard or alerts, and its hand-neuropathy sensor model was not better than chance. — [JMIR 2025;27:e65615](https://www.jmir.org/2025/1/e65615)
- Nearest in form: Neu Health gives Parkinson's patients guided smartphone motor, speech and cognitive tests with composite scores and trend reports for clinicians, and holds FDA clearances, but it targets chronic neurodegenerative disease, not post-discharge follow-up. — [Practical Neurology, Aug 2025](https://practicalneurology.com/news/smartphone-based-tremor-monitoring-system-for-people-with-parkinsons-cleared-by-fda/2476039/); [HIT Consultant, Sep 2026](https://hitconsultant.net/2026/09/03/fda-clears-kneu-health-apple-watch-parkinsons-tremor-dyskinesia-monitoring/)
- Floodlight MS has gamified dexterity tests (pinching, shape drawing) validated against the 9-Hole Peg Test, in MS only. — [Multiple Sclerosis News Today, 2021](https://multiplesclerosisnewstoday.com/news-posts/2021/08/19/ms-smartphone-app-floodlight-shown-reliably-measure-patient-data/)
- Stroke rehab-game platforms (MindMotion GO, Neofect, Motus Nova, Flint) deliver therapy and show therapists usage/performance, need dedicated hardware, and in the sources opened do not describe routine symptom reporting or deterioration alerts; MindMotion GO collected patient-reported measures only at start and end. — [JMIR Serious Games 2025](https://games.jmir.org/2025/1/e69335); [Neofect](https://www.neofect.com/us/neofect-telerehab); [Motus Nova](https://motusnova.com/clinician/); [Flint Rehab](https://www.flintrehab.com/clinical-evidence/)
- Oncology ePRO systems capture neuropathy by self-report only; Kaiku's colorectal study shows self-report brought neuropathy detection forward without changing dosing decisions. — [Elekta news, 2023](https://www.elekta.com/products/life-sciences/elekta-kaiku/news/new-scientific-evidence/)
- General virtual-ward platforms are built around vital signs and questionnaires; the NICE description of the category contains no functional motor testing. — [NICE HTG697 ch. 2](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/HTG697/chapter/2-the-technology)
- Carpal tunnel release: the published smartphone follow-up is a single virtual visit (wound photo, one nerve question, motion video), not longitudinal monitoring. — [Tofte et al., 2020](https://iro.uiowa.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Postoperative-care-via-smartphone-following-carpal/9984001230402771)
- Stroke research prototypes head the same way but are not products: NEST (app games + smartwatch + coach; clinicians asked for under-exercise notifications) and a multimodal smart-home platform using pressure insoles, cameras and an LLM agent that notifies carers (n = 20 for satisfaction; no patient-reported symptoms or clinician dashboard described). — [JMIR Rehabil Assist Technol 2026](https://rehab.jmir.org/2026/1/e79382); [arXiv 2411.19000](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.19000)

### Inferences
- Defensible differentiation statement: existing tools either ask patients how they feel (ePRO/virtual wards) or train/measure movement with dedicated hardware in chronic disease (rehab games, MS/Parkinson's biomarkers); the team's product does both in a browser during the weeks after discharge and alerts on change from the patient's own baseline.
- The white space is real but partly explained by difficulty, not oversight: NeuroDetect's weak hand result, Floodlight's balance test failing to match the clinic test, and the absence of validated thresholds all show that hardware-free motor measures are noisy. The pitch should present the score as a within-patient trend signal that prompts a clinician look, not as a diagnostic measurement.
- "No product found" means none surfaced in about 50 web searches and about 70 page reads, almost all in English (a few French and German pages); it is not proof that none exists (for example in Asian markets or unpublished hospital pilots).

### Gaps
- No systematic patent or app-store search was done.
- Whether Neu Health, MindMaze Therapeutics or Kaiku plan stroke/CIPN motor-monitoring modules is unknown.
- GripAble's clinician portal and remote-monitoring features were not read.

## 5. Typical adherence rates for daily vs weekly check-ins, and recommended frequency/length

### Takeaway
Weekly check-ins with reminders reach roughly 65% to 90% completion; daily check-ins typically run at 55% to 80% early and decay over months. Daily or alternate-day sampling catches far more severe episodes, so short daily check-ins over a limited post-discharge window (10 to 30 days in surgical programmes) are the best-supported compromise.

### Cited Findings
- Weekly, inside a cluster-randomised trial: 91.5% of expected surveys completed in PRO-TECT (20,565 of 22,486). — [OncoDaily summary of Nat Med 2025](https://oncodaily.com/insight/ethan-basch-241583)
- Weekly, single centre: 64.7% average compliance over 18 weeks in eRAPID; higher compliance went with better outcomes and with clinicians actually using the data. — [ASCO Post, 2021](https://ascopost.com/news/january-2021/electronic-patient-self-reporting-of-adverse-events-during-cancer-chemotherapy-erapid-system/)
- A JAMA Network Open commentary puts typical weekly-monitoring completion at 80% to 85% and notes the daily MSK programme fell well below that. — [Rocque, 2022](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2789621)
- Daily, no reminders, cancer: 3.9 of 7 days in the first six months (about 56%, my calculation), 2.7 of 7 after a year (about 39%, my calculation); 57.8% of submissions came between 9 am and 5 pm. — [Daly et al., 2022](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2789617)
- Daily, with an integrated programme, heart failure: 81.2% in month 1, 63.1% in month 12. The same paper cites other home-telehealth adherence ranging 40% to 90% and 64.1% for scheduled daily vital signs in another study. — [Ware et al., 2019](https://mhealth.jmir.org/2019/2/e13259)
- Across ePRO RCTs in cancer, adherence ranged 50% to 100%. — [Perry et al., JMIR 2024](https://www.jmir.org/2024/1/e49089)
- What lower frequency costs (31 chemotherapy patients, daily 19-item symptom inventory as reference): sampling every second day would miss a median 14.6% of severe-symptom episodes, every third day 27.8%, weekly 55.6%. — [Innominato et al., Support Care Cancer 2021 (Warwick repository)](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/152002)
- Post-surgical programmes mostly use daily check-ins; the MSK surgical tracker runs for 10 days; most post-surgical digital interventions cover the first 30 days. — [News-Medical, Oct 2026](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20261019/Postoperative-monitoring-apps-reduce-emergency-visits-and-complications.aspx); [Simon et al., 2021](https://synapse.mskcc.org/synapse/works/186153); [Healthcare IT News](https://www.healthcareitnews.com/news/digital-health-tools-can-be-useful-post-surgery-more-high-quality-research-needed)
- Length: ESMO notes successful weekly systems used 10 to 20 items and advises keeping items few and wording short; it gives no time limit. A Kaiku user reports about two minutes per session. — [ESMO guideline](https://iris.unito.it/retrieve/e27ce435-691b-2581-e053-d805fe0acbaa/PIIS0923753422006913.pdf); [Elekta product page](https://elekta.com/products/oncology-informatics/elekta-one/real-world-outcomes/patient-companion/epros)
- Reminders are a guideline-grade requirement [I, A], and the lowest daily adherence above came from the programme that sent none. — [ESMO guideline](https://iris.unito.it/retrieve/e27ce435-691b-2581-e053-d805fe0acbaa/PIIS0923753422006913.pdf); [Daly et al., 2022](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2789617)
- Reviewers of post-acute monitoring state that the optimal monitoring frequency is unknown and that frequent or hard-to-use systems may lower adherence. — [Cheng et al., JMIR 2025](https://www.jmir.org/2025/1/e64753)
- Stroke home training shows patients will sustain game-based sessions: about 120 minutes a week for 19 weeks among completers in the MindMotion GO cohort. — [JMIR Serious Games 2025](https://games.jmir.org/2025/1/e69335)

### Inferences
- Design target the team can justify: a daily check-in of a few questions plus short games, taking two to five minutes, with a push reminder, for the first 2 to 4 weeks after discharge, then stepping down to alternate days or weekly. Expect 60% to 80% day-level completion at first and plan the trend algorithm to tolerate missing days.
- Because alternate-day sampling loses only about 15% of severe episodes versus over half for weekly, "at least every second day" is a reasonable minimum when daily is too burdensome.
- Missing data should itself be a soft signal (auto-reminder first, human contact only after several missed days), following the Dutch finding that inactivity alerts dominate volume and can be automated.

### Gaps
- No head-to-head randomised comparison of daily vs weekly check-ins was found; the figures above come from different populations and programmes.
- No adherence data exist for daily game-based motor assessments in the first weeks after stroke discharge, carpal tunnel release or during neurotoxic chemotherapy; the NeuroDetect cohort was paid and assessed only every few weeks.
- No source gave an evidence-based maximum session length in minutes.
